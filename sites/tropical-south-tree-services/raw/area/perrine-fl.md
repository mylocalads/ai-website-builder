## Tree Care in Perrine — What Homeowners Need to Know

Perrine is an unincorporated community in south Miami-Dade County, situated between Cutler Bay to the east and the agricultural corridor to the west, with Palmetto Bay to the north and Homestead to the south. Unlike incorporated villages like Palmetto Bay or Pinecrest, Perrine does not have its own municipal government or local tree ordinance — tree work here falls entirely under Miami-Dade County DERM jurisdiction, which simplifies the permit process in some ways while still requiring careful attention to protected species.

### Neighborhoods and Local Tree Character

Perrine's residential neighborhoods are a mix of older post-war housing stock and more recent development, with a tree canopy that reflects decades of tropical growth largely unconstrained by HOA rules. Neighborhoods like West Perrine, Goulds, and the areas surrounding SW 168th Street and US-1 have dense residential blocks where mature mango, avocado, ficus, and gumbo limbo trees are common. Many of these trees were planted informally — not as part of a planned landscape — and have grown into rooflines, driveways, and neighboring properties over time. Root systems in Perrine's sandy soil can spread aggressively, especially near older septic systems and drainage infrastructure that serves much of the area.

### Agricultural Transition and Mixed-Use Properties

Perrine sits at the northern edge of Miami-Dade's agricultural corridor. Some properties in the area include mixed-use lots with ornamental trees alongside productive fruit trees — mango, avocado, lychee, and citrus are common. Agricultural properties and larger lots in the western sections of Perrine may have tree situations that differ significantly from standard residential jobs — larger root masses, denser vegetation, and proximity to agricultural drainage infrastructure. We handle these jobs regularly and bring the right equipment for larger clearing and removal work.

### Storm History and Hurricane Preparedness

Perrine's position in south Miami-Dade puts it in the path of storms moving up from the Florida Keys and the open Atlantic. Hurricane Irma in 2017 caused widespread damage throughout the area — fallen trees, blocked streets, and damage to older homes with trees that had not been maintained through previous storm seasons. Because many of Perrine's trees grew without regular structural pruning, they often carry excessive canopy weight on extended limbs that become projectiles in high winds. Pre-season pruning before June 1 is one of the most effective steps Perrine homeowners can take to reduce storm damage risk, and it is almost always less expensive than emergency cleanup after the fact.

### Permit Requirements in Perrine

As an unincorporated community, Perrine falls directly under Miami-Dade County DERM tree removal regulations with no additional municipal layer. This means the permit process is more straightforward than in incorporated villages like Palmetto Bay or Pinecrest — but protected species still require permits, and removal without the proper approval can result in significant fines. We identify which trees on your property are protected during the site visit, handle the permit application when required, and make sure the work is documented correctly from start to finish.
