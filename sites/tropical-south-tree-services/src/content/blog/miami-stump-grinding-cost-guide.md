Stump grinding in Miami-Dade typically runs **$100 to $450 per stump**. Where you land in that band comes down to diameter, root depth, and access.

The more useful question is usually the one people ask second: is it worth doing at all, or can the stump just stay?

## What moves the price

**Diameter.** The single biggest factor. Grinding time scales with the volume of wood, and a 36-inch trunk is not twice the work of an 18-inch one — it is closer to four times.

**Root depth and spread.** Species matters here. A palm stump is comparatively contained. A mature ficus has a root plate that is an entirely different proposition.

**Access.** If the grinder can be driven to the stump, it is straightforward. If it has to come through a side gate, over a pool deck or across a lawn we are trying not to rut, that takes longer.

**Whether we are already there.** Grinding added to a removal we are doing anyway is meaningfully cheaper than a separate visit, because the mobilisation is already paid for. If you are having a tree taken out, decide about the stump at the quote rather than a month later.

## Why "below grade" is the part that matters

There is a real difference between grinding a stump flush with the ground and grinding it out.

Flush looks finished on the day. Then the grass never quite establishes over it, anything you plant there struggles, and in a good number of South Florida species the root system keeps throwing up suckers for years.

Ground properly — below the soil line, with the visible root flare taken out — the void can be backfilled and the ground genuinely reused.

## What a stump left in place actually does

**It is a trip hazard.** Obvious in a lawn, worse once grass grows over it.

**It feeds termites and decay fungi.** A decaying stump within a few feet of your house is an active food source, and Miami-Dade does not lack for termites.

**It keeps sprouting.** Ficus is the worst offender. Cut the trunk and leave the roots and you get a thicket of suckers to deal with every few weeks, indefinitely.

**It blocks everything.** You cannot plant, pave, build or lay sod over it, so it constrains what you do with that part of the property until it comes out — and it costs the same to grind in three years as it does today.

## What you are left with afterwards

Two things, and it is better to know in advance.

**A lot of chips.** More than most people expect — grinding produces a surprising volume. They can stay as mulch, go into the green waste, be shovelled back into the hole to decay down, or leave with us.

**A hole.** You have just removed all the volume that was filling it. If you are replanting or laying sod, bring in soil and fill it level rather than backfilling with chips, because decaying chips settle and tie up nitrogen in the soil around them.

Most stumps are a few hours. Even the large ones rarely take a full day, which surprises people who have been putting it off for years.
