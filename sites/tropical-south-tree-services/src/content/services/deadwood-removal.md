## Why it matters

A dead limb is a liability with no upside. It contributes nothing to the tree, it is an entry point for decay into the parent limb, and it is the piece most likely to fail first in wind.

If it is suspended over a driveway, a patio, a pool deck or a pavement, it is also a genuine safety and liability question — particularly for HOAs and commercial properties, where "we did not know it was there" is not much of a defence.

## What we take out

- Dead and dying limbs throughout the canopy, cut back to the branch collar so the tree can seal the wound.
- Hangers and broken limbs lodged in the canopy after a storm.
- Dead palm fronds and seed pods, which fall heavy and unpredictably.
- Stubs and old breaks left behind by previous bad pruning.

## What it is not

Deadwood removal takes out what is already dead. It is not a thinning, and it does not reduce the tree's size — the live canopy is left exactly as it is.

That makes it a comparatively quick and inexpensive job, and it is often the honest answer when a homeowner has been quoted a full trim they do not actually need. If your tree is fundamentally healthy and the problem is the dead material in it, this is the job.
