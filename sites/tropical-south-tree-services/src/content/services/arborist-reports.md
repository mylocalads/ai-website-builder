## When you need one

**County permit applications.** Miami-Dade requires permits to remove protected and heritage species, and supporting documentation from a qualified arborist is frequently part of that.

**Insurance claims.** After a failure, an insurer generally wants an independent assessment of cause and condition. Whether the tree was already defective is usually the question the claim turns on.

**HOA and neighbour disputes.** Where responsibility for a tree, or its condition, is contested, a written assessment moves the discussion off opinion.

**Real estate transactions.** A mature tree with a structural defect close to the house is a material issue, and better identified before closing than after.

**Construction and development.** Which trees on a site are protected, what has to be preserved, and what root protection is required during the build.

**Risk assessment.** A formal evaluation of failure likelihood and consequence for a specific tree, which is what a commercial owner or board needs on file.

## What a report covers

Species identification, size and condition. The structural issues present and what they mean. An assessment of risk — how likely failure is, and what it would land on. A recommendation, with the alternatives to removal considered honestly. Photographic documentation. And the arborist's credentials, because a report is only as good as who signed it.

## Why it goes through an ISA certified arborist

Because the people who read these reports check.

Miami-Dade, insurers, attorneys and HOA boards all give weight to the ISA credential specifically. A report from an uncertified contractor — including from us, without our arborist partners — does not carry the same standing, and there is no point paying for a document that will not do its job.

We are straight about this: we are a tree service, and the certified arborists we work with are independent. That separation is exactly what makes the report worth having.
