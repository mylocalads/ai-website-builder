## What a chipper actually does for the job

Whole branches are mostly air. A pile of them fills a truck long before it reaches its weight limit, which is why crews without a chipper end up making three trips or, more often, leaving the pile at your kerb.

Chipping as we cut collapses that volume dramatically. The practical effect for you is that the debris leaves on the day the work is done rather than waiting on a return visit or on the county's storm-debris rounds.

## Keep the chips or lose them

**Keep them.** Fresh chips make good mulch — they hold moisture in South Florida's dry season, moderate soil temperature and suppress weeds. Spread them a few inches deep over beds and around trees, and keep them off the trunk itself.

**Lose them.** If you have nowhere to put them, they go in the truck with everything else at no extra charge.

One caveat we will always mention: do not dig fresh chips into a planting bed. As they break down they tie up nitrogen in the soil, and anything planted into them struggles. On top as a mulch layer they are excellent; mixed in, they are not.
