## When does a tree need to be removed?

Removal is the last option, not the first. In Miami-Dade the situations that genuinely call for it are fairly consistent:

- The tree has been severely damaged by a hurricane or tropical storm and its structure will not recover.
- Disease or pest infestation has compromised the trunk or major limbs.
- Roots are threatening a foundation, a septic system, a driveway or underground utilities.
- The tree is simply too close to a structure to be maintained safely for the long term.
- It is dead, or dying to the point that it is now a liability rather than an asset.

If none of those apply, a crown reduction or a canopy thinning is usually the cheaper and better answer, and we would rather sell you that.

## Permits are part of the job

Miami-Dade County requires a permit to remove protected and heritage tree species. That is not a formality — removing one without approval carries real penalties, and the paperwork is where most homeowners get stuck.

We handle the coordination with the county, and we will tell you honestly at the quote stage whether your tree needs a permit before anything is cut. Where the county requires an assessment first, we can arrange that through our ISA certified arborist partners.

## What is included with every removal

**Safe, controlled take-down.** Our crew uses the right equipment for the situation — bucket lift, tracked spider lift, or climbing — to remove trees of any size, including trees tight against structures, power lines, roofs and fences. Every removal is planned before the first cut.

**Full debris hauling.** Trunk sections, branches, leaves and chips all leave with us. There is no pile at the curb waiting on a second trip, and no separate charge for the cleanup.

**Stump grinding available.** Stump removal can be added to any removal. We grind below grade so the ground is level and ready to replant or sod.

**Same-day response when it is urgent.** For hazardous trees and storm damage we move fast across Miami-Dade. Call and Rafael will assess it personally.

## Species we remove

We work on every species common to South Florida — royal and coconut palms, queen palms, live oak, mango, avocado, gumbo limbo, ficus, black olive and more. Palms and hardwoods come down very differently, and the crew is equipped for both.
