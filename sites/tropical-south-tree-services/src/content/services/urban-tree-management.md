## What a programme includes

**Inventory.** What is on the property, where, what species, what size, what condition. You cannot budget for what nobody has counted.

**Scheduled inspection.** Trees are assessed on a cycle rather than when somebody notices something. That is what turns a large removal into a small pruning job two years earlier.

**Risk assessment.** Structural defects, deadwood over walkways and parking, root conflicts with pavements and utilities — identified and prioritised, with the genuinely urgent separated from the merely untidy.

**Programmed maintenance.** Pruning, thinning and palm work scheduled through the year, with storm-prep work timed ahead of the season rather than into it.

**Storm response.** Existing accounts are prioritised when a storm hits, which is the point at which having a contract stops being an abstraction.

**Documentation.** Records of what was inspected, what was recommended and what was done — which is exactly what a board, an insurer or an attorney will ask for.

## Why it costs less than reactive work

Reactive tree care is more expensive, and not by a small margin.

A defect caught early is a pruning job. The same defect found after failure is an emergency removal, plus whatever it landed on, plus the deductible, plus the disruption. Emergency work also carries emergency pricing, and after a named storm it carries a two-week wait as well.

Programmed work is scheduled in normal conditions at normal rates, and it is a predictable line in a budget rather than a surprise that has to be found mid-year.

## Liability

For commercial and HOA properties this is the part that matters most.

If a limb comes down on a resident's car or a customer's head, the question that follows is whether the owner knew or should have known about the defect. A documented inspection programme is a materially better answer than nothing at all.
