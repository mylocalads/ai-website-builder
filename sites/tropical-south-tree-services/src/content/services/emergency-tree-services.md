## What counts as an emergency

Call immediately, at any hour, if:

- A tree or large limb has come down on your house, garage, car, fence or pool cage.
- A tree is leaning, split, or has visibly lifted its root plate since the storm.
- A limb is hung up in the canopy — "widowmakers" drop without warning and never at a convenient moment.
- Anything is in contact with, or resting near, the power service drop.
- A fallen tree is blocking your driveway, your access, or a shared road in a community.

If a line is involved, call Florida Power & Light first and keep everyone well clear. We do not cut anything energised, and neither should anybody else.

## How an emergency call runs

**We answer.** Rafael takes emergency calls directly and will talk through what you are looking at before anyone is dispatched.

**Make safe first.** The initial priority is removing the immediate danger — taking the load off a structure, bringing down a hung limb, opening access. That is not always the same as finishing the whole job in one visit, and we will be straight with you about which is which.

**Then the full cleanup.** Once the site is stable we section out, chip and haul everything. Driveways, walkways and roads are cleared and blown off before we leave.

**Documentation for your claim.** We photograph the damage and the work as we go. If you are filing an insurance claim, that record matters, and where an assessment is needed we can arrange one through our ISA certified arborist partners.

## After a named storm

Demand goes vertical for about a fortnight after a hurricane crosses Miami-Dade — every crew in the county is committed and the wait becomes real regardless of who you call.

We triage on genuine risk: trees on houses and blocked emergency access first, cosmetic cleanup after. If your situation is dangerous, say so on the call and be specific about what is under load.
