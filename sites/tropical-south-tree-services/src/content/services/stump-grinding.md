## Why grinding matters after a removal

Cutting a tree down at the base leaves you with the part that causes the problems.

A stump is a trip hazard in a lawn and an obstacle to anything you want to plant, pave or build. It is an active food source for termites and decay fungi within a few feet of the house. And in a good number of South Florida species — ficus especially — the root system keeps sending up suckers long after the trunk is gone, so you are back to cutting shoots every few weeks.

## What "below grade" actually means

The grinder's rotating wheel cuts the stump wood into chips, and the operator works it across the whole stump and the visible root flare until the wood is taken well beneath the soil line.

That produces two things you should expect:

**A lot of chips** — more than most people anticipate. They can stay on site as mulch, go into the green waste, or leave with us.

**A hole** — because the volume that filled it has just been ground out. You can shovel the chips back in to decay down, or bring in soil to bring it level with the surrounding ground, which is what you want if you intend to sod or replant over it.

## Timing

Most stump removal is fast. Even large stumps are usually a few hours rather than a day, and grinding is normally quoted alongside a removal rather than as a separate visit.

We always offer grinding when we take a tree down, and we will explain what leaving the stump in place actually costs you over the following couple of years.
