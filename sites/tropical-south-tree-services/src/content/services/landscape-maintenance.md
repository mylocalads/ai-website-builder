## A schedule, not a rescue

South Florida does not have a growing season — it has twelve months of one. A property left to a couple of visits a year is never actually maintained; it is repeatedly recovered, and it looks it.

Maintenance here means a crew on a set day: mowing to the correct height for the turf rather than scalping it, edging the hard lines, trimming the beds back off the paths, and blowing every hardscape surface clean before they go.

## What the round covers

- **Mowing** — cut to the right height for the species and the season. Blades are sharpened regularly; a dull blade tears St. Augustine rather than cutting it and you can see the difference within a week.
- **Edging** — driveways, walkways, bed lines and pool decks.
- **Trimming** — shrubs and beds kept off the paths, the walls and the windows.
- **Blowing** — every hard surface cleared, with nothing blown into beds or the pool.

## Beyond the weekly round

- **Landscape design** — planting plans that account for mature size, root habit and salt exposure rather than what looked good at the nursery.
- **Planting** — installed properly, with the root flare at grade and a watering schedule that gets it through establishment.
- **Mulching** — two to four inches, out toward the drip line, and never piled against a trunk.
- **Weeding flower beds** — kept on top of as part of the round, not as an emergency.
- **Spring clean-ups** — the reset before the wet season, and the right moment for structural work in the beds.

## One crew for the whole property

Most Miami-Dade properties need two things at once: the grass cut on a schedule and the trees kept off the roof. Splitting that between two companies is how homeowners end up with nobody responsible for anything.

We already handle the tree side — removal, trimming, stump grinding and storm response — so the maintenance crew is working a property the company knows.
