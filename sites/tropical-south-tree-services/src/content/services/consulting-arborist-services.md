## What consulting covers

**Diagnosis.** A tree in decline has a cause — root damage, soil compaction, drainage, pest, pathogen, or something done to it years ago. Treating the symptom without identifying the cause wastes money and usually loses the tree anyway.

**Preservation planning.** Which trees on a site are worth keeping, and what has to happen during construction to keep them. Most construction-related tree deaths are caused by root-zone damage and compaction during the build, and they show up two or three years later when responsibility is long settled.

**Expert opinion.** For attorneys, insurers and boards — an independent view on condition, cause of failure, or standard of care.

**Second opinions.** If you have been told a healthy-looking tree must come out, it is entirely reasonable to have that checked by someone with no financial interest in the removal.

**Appraisal.** Mature trees carry real value, which matters in insurance claims and property disputes.

## Why the independence matters

There is an obvious conflict of interest in a tree company diagnosing a problem it would be paid to solve.

We do not pretend otherwise. Consulting work is coordinated through ISA certified arborists who are independent of our crew, and their recommendation is theirs — including when it is that the tree is fine and nothing needs doing.

That is the only arrangement under which a second opinion means anything.

## Getting a second opinion

If you have a quote for a large removal and something about it does not sit right, get it checked. A consultation costs a fraction of an unnecessary removal, and a mature shade tree is not replaceable in any timeframe that matters to you.

The reverse also happens, and matters more: a tree that looks fine from the ground can have a structural defect that a qualified assessment finds. Both are worth knowing before a storm decides for you.
