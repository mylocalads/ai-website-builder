## How it works

Tell us at the quote stage. It has to be decided before the work rather than after, because once a trunk has gone through the process of being sectioned for haul-off it is not much use as firewood.

We cut to whatever length suits your fireplace or fire pit — sixteen inches is standard — and stack it where you want it on the property. Splitting is not included; rounds are what you get, and they season better as rounds anyway.

## What burns well here

Not all of it is worth keeping.

**Worth it:** live oak and mahogany are dense, burn hot and long, and are genuinely good firewood. Both are common removals in Miami-Dade.

**Adequate:** mango and avocado burn fine once properly seasoned.

**Not worth it:** palm is not firewood. It is fibrous, holds water for a very long time, burns poorly and produces little heat. Ficus and Australian pine are similarly not worth the storage space.

We will tell you honestly which category your tree falls into rather than stacking a cord of palm trunk in your garden.

## Seasoning

Freshly cut wood in South Florida is wet, and in this humidity it takes longer to dry than most people expect — six months to a year for hardwood, stacked off the ground with the air able to move through it and the top covered.

Burning it green is how chimneys accumulate creosote. If you want wood for this winter, it needed cutting last one.
