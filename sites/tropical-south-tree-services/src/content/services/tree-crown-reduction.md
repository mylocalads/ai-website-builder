## When reduction is the right call

- The tree has grown into the roofline, the gutters or the pool cage.
- It is crowding the power service drop or a neighbour's property.
- Long lateral limbs have become end-heavy and are at risk of tearing out at the union.
- It has outgrown the space it was planted in, but is otherwise healthy and worth keeping.

Reduction is very often the alternative to removal. If the tree is sound and the problem is purely one of size, taking it down is the expensive answer to a solvable problem.

## Reduction is not topping

This is the distinction that matters most, and it is the one cheap crews blur.

**Reduction** cuts back to a lateral branch at least a third the diameter of the limb being removed. That lateral takes over, the wound seals, and the tree keeps its natural form at a smaller size.

**Topping** cuts limbs back to stubs with no regard for where the next branch is. The tree responds with a burst of fast, weakly attached shoots around the wound. Within a few seasons it is bushier than before, structurally worse, and considerably more likely to fail in a storm — and the decay that entered through the open stubs is permanent.

Miami-Dade County's own guidance tells homeowners not to allow topping. If a quote sounds cheap and involves "taking the top off", this is what is being sold.

## What to expect

Reduction is slower and more skilled than tipping the ends off, so it costs more per tree than a hatrack would. What you get is a tree that is the size you need, stays that shape for years, and does not need re-cutting every season to control the regrowth.
