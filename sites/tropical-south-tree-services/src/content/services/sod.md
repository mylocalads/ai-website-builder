## What sod actually is

Sod is grass together with a thin layer of the soil beneath it, held together by its own root system. It is laid straight onto prepared ground and knits in over the following weeks.

Beyond appearance it does real work: it cools the immediate area, improves air and water quality, and helps with local flooding by draining water into the ground rather than sending it into already overtaxed storm systems.

## Installation and replacement

**Installation** is new ground — a bare lot, a cleared area, or the spot where a tree and stump have just come out.

**Replacement** means an existing lawn that is past recovery. That is more work than it sounds: the failed turf has to come up and leave the site before anything new goes down, and it is worth diagnosing why the old one died first. A lawn that failed from shade, drainage or compaction will kill its replacement the same way.

## How we lay it

We measure the area and prepare the ground first — old turf and debris cleared, grade corrected where it needs to be. The sod then goes down tight, seam to seam, and is rolled so it makes proper contact with the soil underneath. Air gaps under a seam are where a new lawn dies first.

## The first month decides it

New sod needs deep, regular watering while the roots move down into the soil beneath. The common failure is watering lightly and often rather than deeply and less often — that encourages a shallow root system that cannot survive its first dry week.
