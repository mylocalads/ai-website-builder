## Choosing the right tree

The questions that matter here are not the ones most people are asked at a garden centre:

**How big does it get, really?** The single most common problem we are called to fix is a tree planted ten feet from a house that was always going to be forty feet wide.

**How does it handle wind?** Some species are notably storm-resilient in South Florida — live oak, gumbo limbo, mahogany. Others fail predictably and take the fence with them.

**What are the roots going to do?** Ficus in particular will find a foundation, a pool shell, a septic system or a driveway, and it will win.

**Is it protected, or is it invasive?** Miami-Dade regulates both. Planting a listed invasive is a problem you are creating for yourself.

**Salt, soil and water table.** A coastal lot in Cutler Bay and an inland one in Kendall West are genuinely different growing conditions.

## Planting it properly

Most tree failures in the first two years come down to installation, not the tree.

We dig a hole wide rather than deep, set the root flare at grade instead of burying it, backfill with native soil rather than a rich mix that discourages roots from leaving the hole, and mulch clear of the trunk instead of piling it against the bark.

Staking is used only where the tree genuinely needs it, and we tell you when to take it off. A tree left staked too long never develops the trunk taper that lets it stand up on its own.

## Establishment

The first year decides the outcome. New trees need deep, regular watering while the roots move out into the surrounding soil, and the failure mode is almost always a tree that was watered lightly and often rather than deeply and less often.

You get a schedule when we finish, and it is worth following literally.
