## Agricultural scale

Redland is not suburban tree work. Acreage parcels, working groves and nurseries, long fence lines and windbreaks, and land that in places has not been cleared in years.

The difference is mostly one of capacity rather than technique. A job here needs more chipping capacity, more haul-off, and typically a full day or several rather than a scheduled couple of hours. We are equipped for it — our own chipper and chip truck, and lifts that work on unpaved ground rather than needing a driveway.

## Groves and windbreaks

Grove work has its own logic. Rows that have to stay workable for equipment, trees pruned for production rather than ornament, and windbreak lines whose whole purpose is taking wind load — which means they need to be maintained to actually do that rather than left to become a debris source.

Invasive species are a persistent problem on agricultural and cleared land here. Brazilian pepper and melaleuca colonise disturbed ground faster than anything native, and half-removing them makes the problem worse rather than better.

## Different permitting rules

This is where Redland genuinely differs from the rest of the county.

On agricultural land, grove trees and specimen trees — generally those with a **trunk diameter of 18 inches or more** — can follow different provisions than a standard residential removal, depending on how the parcel is classified.

Land classification is the deciding factor and it is not always obvious from the deed. We work out what applies to your parcel before quoting, and handle the county coordination from there.
