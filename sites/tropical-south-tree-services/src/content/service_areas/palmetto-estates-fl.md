## Sixty years of growth

Much of Palmetto Estates was built in the 1950s and 60s, and a good proportion of the trees went in not long after.

That means specimens with sixty-odd years on them — live oak, royal palm, avocado and mango — on lots and around infrastructure designed when they were saplings. Driveways, walkways, sewer laterals and septic systems were all installed around trees a fraction of their current size.

The consequence is that root conflict is a much bigger share of our work here than in newer communities: lifted paving, cracked drives, and roots into laterals. Some of that is solvable with root pruning and hardscape work; some of it means the tree has genuinely outgrown the site.

## Fruit trees

Avocado and mango are everywhere in this community and they are worth treating properly rather than as generic canopy.

Both benefit from pruning for structure and light rather than being cut back hard, and both fruit better for it. A mango that has been hatracked will sulk for years.

## Permits in Palmetto Estates

Palmetto Estates is unincorporated, so tree removal permits are handled **directly through Miami-Dade County** rather than a city office.

Protected and heritage trees require a permit before removal. We identify which trees on your property are protected, prepare and submit the paperwork, and coordinate with DERM on your behalf.
