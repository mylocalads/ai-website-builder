## A canopy that all grew up together

Country Walk was one of the communities worst hit by Hurricane Andrew in 1992, and most of what stands today went up in the rebuild that followed.

That produces something unusual: a neighbourhood where the trees are nearly all the same age. Three decades of growth on landscaping installed at roughly the same moment, which means whole streets arriving at the same stage together.

That stage is the one that matters. At thirty years these trees are large enough to do real damage in a storm, and they are at exactly the point where structural pruning determines whether the next thirty years are an asset or a series of removals.

## HOA-governed

Much of Country Walk sits under association governance, with landscape standards covering approved species, maintenance requirements and removal approvals.

We work to those specifications and are used to coordinating with property managers and boards. County approval and association approval are separate — you need both, and we will be clear about which is which.

## Permits in Country Walk

Country Walk is in unincorporated Miami-Dade County, so tree removal permits are handled **through the county directly** rather than a city permitting office.

The county's protected tree ordinance applies to any heritage or protected species on your lot. We advise on whether your specific tree needs a permit before any work starts, and handle the paperwork.
