## Big trees, and the standard they demand

Palmetto Bay is a short run north of our yard, and it is one of the places where the difference between a real tree crew and a truck with a chainsaw shows up fastest.

The village's canopy — live oak, mahogany, gumbo limbo and royal palm through Coral Reef Estates, Old Cutler Bay and the waterfront streets — includes trees that have been growing for half a century. A poorly placed cut on a specimen like that is not something the tree grows out of.

## Irma, and hidden damage

Palmetto Bay sustained significant tree damage in Hurricane Irma in 2017, and a good deal of what we still find here traces back to that storm.

Trees that look entirely healthy from the ground routinely carry damage from that week — cracked branch unions that have closed over on the outside, broken stubs now hidden by regrowth, and root plates that were loosened in saturated soil and never fully re-anchored.

That is the argument for having mature trees looked at properly rather than judged from the driveway. A pre-season inspection catches the defects a storm has already started.

## What we do most here

Structural pruning on mature hardwoods. Canopy thinning ahead of the season on the big dense specimens near houses. Royal palm work, which needs doing correctly rather than aggressively. And removals where a tree genuinely has to go, taken down in sections so nothing swings into a pool cage or a neighbouring roof.

## Permits in Palmetto Bay

Removals fall under **Miami-Dade County DERM**. Given how much mature protected canopy the village holds, permit requirements come up here more often than almost anywhere else we work.

We identify protected trees before quoting, handle the county coordination, and arrange written assessments through our ISA certified arborist partners where the application requires one.
