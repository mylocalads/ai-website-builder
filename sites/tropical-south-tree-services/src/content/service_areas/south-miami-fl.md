## Old trees, block by block

South Miami's residential character shifts noticeably street to street.

**Ponce-Davis and the streets around the University of Miami** hold some of the oldest and largest canopy trees anywhere in Miami-Dade. Live oak, mahogany and gumbo limbo growing for fifty to eighty years, most of which falls squarely under the county's protected-species rules.

Trees of that age are genuinely valuable — to the property, to the street, and in the county's view. They are also the ones where bad pruning is least recoverable.

## Two approvals, not one

South Miami has one of the more active HOA landscapes in the county.

Many communities off Red Road and along SW 57th Avenue have governing documents requiring **board approval before any tree removal or major trimming**, including on private property. That is separate from, and additional to, the county permit.

Getting one and not the other is the common mistake. We handle the DERM side and will tell you clearly what your board still has to approve before we start.

## Permits in South Miami

Miami-Dade County DERM governs removal of protected and heritage species, which covers a large share of the mature live oak and mahogany on these streets.

We identify what is protected, prepare the paperwork, and arrange a written arborist assessment through our ISA certified partners where the application requires it.

## Storm exposure

South Miami sits in the path of the cells that build over the Everglades and push east, as well as the Atlantic tracks.

Irma in 2017 caused significant canopy loss through these residential streets, with widespread oak and mahogany damage. As in Palmetto Bay, much of the damage that remains is internal and invisible from the driveway.
