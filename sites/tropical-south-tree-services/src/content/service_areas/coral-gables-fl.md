## A city built around its trees

Coral Gables was founded in 1925 by George Merrick as one of the first planned communities in the United States. The Mediterranean Revival architecture, the coral rock, and the deliberately planted tropical canopy were all part of one vision — and the city still actively protects the last of those.

Wide boulevards lined with large banyans and live oaks, estate properties with mature specimen trees, and a municipal government that treats the canopy as civic infrastructure rather than landscaping.

## The permitting is the job

Coral Gables administers **its own tree removal permits**, in coordination with Miami-Dade County DERM. That makes it one of the few places in the county with two real layers of approval.

Protected species — live oak, banyan, gumbo limbo, mahogany among others — require a **city permit before any removal work begins**. Replacement tree requirements are commonly enforced, and fines for unpermitted removal are substantial.

We identify what is protected during the quote, prepare the application, and coordinate with the city and the county. Where the application needs a written tree assessment, we arrange it through our ISA certified arborist partners — their reports are written to meet both Coral Gables and DERM requirements.

## Honest answers about what you can do

Some of what homeowners ask for in the Gables is not permissible, and we would rather say so at the quote than halfway through.

If the answer is that your banyan cannot come out, or that the city will require three replacements for it, that is worth knowing before a crew is booked. It is a slower answer than "sure, Tuesday" — and it is the one that does not end in a fine.

## Storm exposure

Coral Gables lost significant canopy in Hurricane Irma in 2017 and has taken repeated hits across a century.

The city's large mature trees are exactly what makes it beautiful and exactly what presents the risk. Annual pre-season inspection before 1 June is genuinely worth doing on any Gables property with big specimen trees near the house.
