## A city of very different tree problems

Miami covers enough ground that the job changes completely depending on the postcode.

**Coconut Grove** has some of the oldest protected trees in Miami-Dade — large live oaks, gumbo limbo and native hardwoods that have been growing for generations. Removing a protected species in the Grove means DERM permits and, very often, mandatory replacement planting. This is the part of the city where doing it in the wrong order gets expensive.

**Coral Way and SW 8th Street** are lined corridors where much of the canopy sits in or near the public right-of-way, which brings City of Miami urban forestry rules into play alongside the county's.

**Residential neighbourhoods** across the city carry the post-Andrew replanting — a generation of trees now large enough to be a genuine consideration in a storm.

## Andrew changed the rules

Hurricane Andrew in 1992 devastated Miami's urban canopy and fundamentally reshaped how Miami-Dade regulates trees. The current ordinance, the protected species list and the replacement requirements all trace back to that storm and the replanting that followed.

The practical consequence for a homeowner today is that the county takes its tree ordinance seriously, and fines for unpermitted removal of a protected tree are not nominal.

## Permits in the City of Miami

Two layers apply, and which one you are in matters.

**On private property**, Miami-Dade County DERM rules govern removal of protected and heritage species.

**In the public right-of-way** or on city-owned land, the City of Miami's own urban forestry regulations apply as well.

We identify which regime covers your tree at the quote stage, coordinate the permit, and arrange a written arborist assessment through our ISA certified partners where the application needs one.
