## Trees reaching the age that matters

Richmond West is newer than most of the communities we serve, and a large share of its trees were installed as part of the original development.

Those trees are now hitting the point where what happens next is decided. Structural pruning in this window — establishing sound branch architecture, correcting narrow unions, clearing rooflines before limbs get heavy — is cheap, and it is what determines whether these are assets or removals in another fifteen years.

It is also the least glamorous work we sell, and the highest return per dollar of anything on this site.

## Two approvals

Richmond West is unincorporated, so removals fall under **Miami-Dade County's tree ordinance** with no city permit office involved.

Many of its communities also have association landscape rules covering species, maintenance and removal approval. We work out what applies on both fronts and handle the county paperwork.

## Storm preparation

West county is exposed to the cells that build over the Everglades and track east through the summer.

Younger trees are not automatically safer in a storm — a poorly structured twenty-year-old tree with a narrow union and a dense canopy will fail before a well-maintained sixty-year-old one. Pre-season thinning and structural correction is worth doing here precisely because the trees are still young enough for it to change the outcome.
