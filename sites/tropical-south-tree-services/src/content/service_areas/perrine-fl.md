## Mature growth on tight lots

Perrine's residential blocks are a mix of older post-war housing stock and more recent building, with a canopy that has grown for decades without much in the way of HOA constraint.

That produces a specific kind of job: large trees on lots that are not large. A ficus or live oak with fifty years of unrestricted growth on a standard residential lot is by definition close to a house, a fence, a driveway or a service line, and often all four.

Most of what we do here is clearance and structural work — getting canopy off rooflines, reducing end-heavy limbs before they tear out, and taking out the trees that were planted somewhere they were never going to fit.

## Simpler permitting

Perrine is unincorporated, which makes this one of the more straightforward parts of the county to work in.

Removals fall **directly under Miami-Dade County DERM**, with no additional municipal layer of the kind that applies in Pinecrest or Coral Gables. There is one authority, one process and one set of rules.

Protected and heritage species still require a permit, and Perrine's mature growth means that comes up frequently. We identify what is protected, handle the county coordination, and arrange written assessments through our ISA certified arborist partners where DERM asks for one.

## Storm exposure

Perrine's position in south Miami-Dade puts it in the path of systems moving up from the Keys and in off the open Atlantic.

Irma in 2017 caused widespread damage through the area — trees down, streets blocked, and property damage across these neighbourhoods. Pre-season work here is not a luxury.
