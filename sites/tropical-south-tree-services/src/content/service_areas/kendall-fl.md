## Working inside HOA rules

A large part of Kendall sits under a homeowners association, and that matters more than people expect.

Associations commonly set which species may be planted, what clearances have to be maintained, whether a removal needs board approval, and what replacement is required. A crew that ignores all that does not save you money — it creates a violation letter and, sometimes, an order to replant at your expense.

We are used to satisfying both the resident and the board, and we will ask about your association's requirements before we quote rather than after.

## Irma's hidden legacy

Kendall took significant canopy damage in Hurricane Irma in 2017, and a lot of what we are called to now is the delayed consequence.

Trees that survived that storm frequently carry structural damage that is not visible from the ground — cracked crotches, broken stub ends that have since closed over, and root plates weakened by waterlogged soil that never fully recovered their anchorage.

Those are the trees that come down in the next storm, and they are entirely findable in advance if somebody actually inspects the canopy.

## Permits in Kendall

Kendall is unincorporated, so removals fall under **Miami-Dade County DERM** with no separate municipal permit office.

Protected species — live oak, gumbo limbo and certain palms — need a permit before removal. We identify which trees on your property are protected, prepare the application and handle the county side.

Worth noting: county approval and HOA approval are two separate things, and you need both. We deal with the county; we will tell you plainly what your association still needs to sign.
