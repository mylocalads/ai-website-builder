Every contractor in South Florida has had this conversation. The quote comes in, and someone asks whether it would be cheaper without the permit.

It is cheaper on the day. It is reliably more expensive later, and here is the mechanism.

## The work does not disappear — the record does

A permit creates a county record that a specific scope of work was done and inspected. Without one, the work still exists on the property; there is simply nothing saying it was done to code. That gap surfaces at predictable moments.

**At resale.** Buyers' agents and title work pull permit history. Open or missing permits on a roof, an addition, an electrical panel or a window package become a negotiating item at best and a closing delay at worst.

**At refinancing or insurance renewal.** A wind mitigation inspection is looking for documented opening protection and roof attachment. Work with no inspection record behind it does not earn the credit, however well it was built.

**At recertification.** Miami-Dade's structural recertification programme brings a professional through older buildings on a schedule. Unpermitted structural, roofing or mechanical work discovered at that point has to be resolved — and the owner at the time is the one resolving it, not whoever had it done.

**At code enforcement.** A neighbour's complaint, a stop-work order, an after-the-fact permit application at a higher fee, and in the worst case remediation of finished work. Opening up completed construction to prove what is behind it costs multiples of what the inspection would have cost.

## After-the-fact permitting is a real process, and not a pleasant one

Miami-Dade will permit work retroactively, but the burden of proof lands on the owner. You may need drawings for work already covered up, engineering letters, exposure of concealed conditions for inspection, and elevated fees. The original contractor is frequently long gone, which means the person assembling that evidence is you.

## What a legitimate contractor handles

On our jobs the permit is not an add-on line item you can decline. We prepare the package, submit it, build to the approved drawings and product approvals, meet the inspectors, and close the permit out so the record shows the work completed. That applies whether it is a re-roof, an AC changeout, a window package, a plumbing re-pipe or an addition.

It is also worth saying plainly: a contractor who offers to skip the permit is telling you something about how they handle everything else. The permit is the one part of the job a third party checks.
