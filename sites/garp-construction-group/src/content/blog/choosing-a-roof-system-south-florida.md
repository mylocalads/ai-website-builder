Most homeowners start a re-roof conversation with a material already in mind, usually because a neighbour just had one done. It is worth backing up a step, because on a lot of South Florida properties the choice is narrower than it looks.

## The structural question comes first

Concrete tile is heavy — several times the dead load of asphalt shingle. If your house was framed for shingle, putting tile on it is not a material swap; it is a structural question, and the answer sometimes involves engineering. Going the other direction, from tile to shingle, is straightforward and is why plenty of Miami-Dade homes have quietly changed system over the years.

So the first honest answer to "which roof should I get" is often "which ones can this structure take", and that is something we establish on the site visit rather than in a brochure.

## What each system is genuinely good at

**Architectural shingle** is the most common re-roof we do. It is the least expensive per square, the fastest to install, and modern architectural product performs well when it is fastened to the approved schedule. Its weakness in this climate is heat and UV: a shingle roof in Miami works harder than the same roof in Georgia.

**Concrete tile — flat or barrel** lasts a long time, handles the sun well, and is what a large share of the housing stock here already has. It costs more, weighs more, and the failure mode people underestimate is the underlayment: the tile can look perfect while the membrane under it has reached end of life. If someone quotes you a "tile repair" without discussing the underlayment, ask why.

**Metal** performs extremely well in this climate and lasts longer than either of the above. It is the highest up-front cost of the sloped options. On the right house it is the best long-run value; on the wrong one it is money spent on a roof that will outlive the rest of the building.

**Flat and low-slope systems** are not really a style choice — they are what the geometry requires. Modified bitumen and single-ply systems each have their place. What matters more than the membrane brand is the detailing at penetrations, drains and terminations, which is where flat roofs actually fail.

## Where the schedule really goes

Homeowners tend to budget time for the labour and forget the paperwork. On most single-family re-roofs the tear-off and install is a matter of days. The permit review and the inspection sequence usually drive the calendar more than the crew does — and it varies by municipality, not just by county.

We quote the realistic permit timeline before you sign rather than after. It is a less comfortable conversation up front and a much better one later.

## What we would actually ask you

- How long do you plan to own the property? A twelve-year horizon and a forty-year horizon point at different systems.
- What does the structure allow without engineering?
- Is there a homeowners association or a design review — Coral Gables, for instance — with a say in profile and colour?
- What is the condition of the deck? Nobody knows until the old roof is off, which is why a good quote tells you how deck replacement is priced rather than pretending it will not come up.
