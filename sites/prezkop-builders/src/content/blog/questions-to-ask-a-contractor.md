Search any Northeastern PA community group for "contractor" and you will find the same posts: someone got ghosted after the estimate, someone is three weeks into a two-week job with no one on site, someone got a final invoice that did not resemble the number they agreed to.

Almost all of it is preventable at the estimate. Here is what to ask.

## 1. Are you registered as a PA Home Improvement Contractor, and what is your number?

Pennsylvania requires it for most residential work over $500. The number is public and you can verify it with the Attorney General's office in about a minute. Ours is **PA164836**. Anyone who cannot produce one on request is telling you something.

## 2. Can I see a current certificate of insurance?

Not "yes we're insured." The certificate, with current dates. If someone gets hurt on your property and the contractor's coverage lapsed, that becomes your problem.

## 3. What exactly is included — and what is not?

This is the one that prevents change-order arguments. A scope that says "build deck" is not a scope. It should name the framing material, the decking material, the railing, the footing detail, whether stairs are included, and who is hauling the debris. The things that are *excluded* matter just as much as the things that are included.

## 4. Who is actually going to be on my property?

Ask whether the person quoting the job will be on it, or whether it goes to a crew you have not met, or to subs. There is nothing wrong with subcontracting specialized trades — electrical and HVAC usually should be. But you should know before day one, not on day one.

## 5. What days will you be here, and what happens when it rains?

Weather is going to move your schedule in this valley. That is not a red flag. Not hearing about it is. Ask what the contractor does when the schedule slips: do they call you, or do you find out by looking out the window at an empty driveway?

## 6. What is the payment schedule tied to?

Payments should be tied to milestones — materials delivered, framing complete, final walkthrough — not to dates on a calendar. And be careful with large deposits before any material has been ordered.

## 7. What happens if something is wrong after you leave?

Ask it plainly. Ask what their warranty covers, how long it runs, and what the process is for getting someone back out. The answer tells you a great deal about whether the business expects to still be here.
