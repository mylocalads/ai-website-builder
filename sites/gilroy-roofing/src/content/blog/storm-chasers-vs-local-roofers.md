Every serious storm in the Wyoming Valley is followed by the same thing: unfamiliar
trucks, out-of-state plates, and someone at your door telling you your roof is
destroyed and they can get insurance to pay for all of it.

Some of those crews are legitimate. Many are not. Here is how to tell.

## Ask how long they have been at that address

A storm-chasing outfit is built to move. It has a phone number, a magnetic door
sign, and a motel room. Ask where their shop is, how long it has been there, and
whose roofs they did in your neighborhood last year. A local contractor answers
those questions immediately, because the answer is the whole business.

## Be careful about who signs what

The most common trap is a document that assigns your insurance claim over to the
contractor before any work is agreed. Once signed, you can lose control over what
gets repaired and what gets paid. You should never have to sign over your claim to
get an inspection or an estimate.

## Watch for the deposit

Asking for money before a shingle comes off is normal for a crew that may not come
back. We have never taken a deposit in fifty-four years of doing this. You pay when
the job is complete and you are satisfied with it.

## Check the manufacturer certification, not just the reviews

Manufacturer certifications like Owens Corning Platinum Preferred are issued to
established contractors and are what make a long material-and-labor warranty
possible. A warranty from a company that leaves the state in November is not worth
the paper it is on.

## If a claim is genuinely warranted, that is fine

None of this means storm damage claims are suspect. They are often completely
legitimate, and we file them for customers regularly. The difference is who is
handling it: someone who will still be reachable in five years when a flashing
detail needs attention, or someone who will not.
