Buford runs from Lake Lanier shoreline properties down to the newer construction along the Mall of Georgia corridor and out to Hamilton Mill, and the lake homes are a genuinely different design problem.

A property on the water has two fronts. There is the elevation people drive up to, and there is the one people see from the dock, the yard and the water — which on most lake houses is the side with the decking, the steps down the slope and the trees worth uplighting. Lighting plans that treat a lake house like a street-facing house light the wrong half of it. We walk both sides before quoting.

Away from the water, the newer subdivisions around the Mall of Georgia corridor have the clean, tall gable geometry that permanent architectural lighting suits well, and several of them are the kind of neighbourhood where a display goes up on one house in November and four more follow.

Permanent roofline systems, landscape and dock-path lighting, holiday displays on the lease programme and event lighting, installed throughout Buford.
