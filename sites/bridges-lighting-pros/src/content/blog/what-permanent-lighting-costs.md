## The number people trade online is per linear foot

If you go looking on homeowner forums you will find the same figure over and over: somewhere around twenty to twenty-five dollars per linear foot, with typical rooflines landing in the low-to-mid thousands. That is a real market signal and it is worth knowing. It is also close to useless as a way to compare two quotes, because "linear foot" quietly hides most of the cost.

## What actually moves the price

**Total roofline footage.** The obvious one, and the only one the per-foot number captures.

**Gables, peaks and direction changes.** A hundred feet of straight eave and a hundred feet broken into six gables are not the same job. Every peak is a termination, an alignment decision and a piece of track cut to fit. Two houses with identical square footage can differ substantially on this alone.

**Single versus multi-storey access.** Second-storey and steep-pitch work needs different equipment and more time, and it is a real safety cost rather than a padding cost.

**Zones.** How many sections do you want to control independently? One zone for the whole house is cheapest. Separating the main roofline, the garage, the porch columns and the landscape lighting into four addressable zones costs more and is dramatically more useful in practice.

**What gets tied in.** Landscape lighting, tree uplighting and column wraps running off the same controller and the same app is the cleanest outcome and worth pricing at the same time — retrofitting it later usually costs more than including it now.

## The questions worth asking any installer

Price is the easy part of the comparison. These are harder and matter more:

- **What does it look like at noon?** Ask to see a daylight photo of a finished install, not a night shot. Colour-matched track with concealed wiring reads as trim. Surface-run cable does not, and no amount of colour at night fixes it.
- **Who comes back?** Controllers fail and connections corrode — that is true of every system on the market. The question is whether service is a local phone call or a warranty claim posted to another state. We respond within 24 hours, next business day, through the holiday season.
- **What grade is the hardware?** Anything living permanently on a Georgia roofline has to take summer heat, storms and the occasional ice event. Commercial-grade components cost more once and less over five years.
- **Who is designing it?** A layout traced off a satellite image and a layout decided at the kerb at dusk produce visibly different houses.
