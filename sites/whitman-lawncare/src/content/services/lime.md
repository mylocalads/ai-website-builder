long_description: "Lime is an essential amendment for maintaining soil health, especially where the ground tends to be acidic or sour — which describes much of Northeast Pennsylvania. Applying lime neutralizes acidic soil and creates a balanced pH conducive to healthy grass growth. That matters because many essential nutrients become chemically unavailable in highly acidic conditions, so an untreated lawn can be fertilized faithfully and still come back thin. Sweetening the soil promotes better nutrient uptake, which improves lawn color and density. Lime also helps manage thatch, the layer of dead grass and organic matter that can suffocate a lawn, by encouraging the soil biology that breaks it down. Healthy pH and available nutrients lead to stronger root systems that withstand drought and winter stress. We test first and apply the right amount rather than guessing."

faqs:
  - q: "How do I know if my lawn needs lime?"
    a: "A soil test is the only reliable way. Acidic soil has no visible signature of its own — it just looks like a lawn that will not respond to fertilizer, which is exactly why so many properties here get over-fertilized instead of corrected."
  - q: "How often should lime be applied?"
    a: "Usually every two to three years in this area, but it depends entirely on your starting pH and soil type. We re-test rather than putting it down on a fixed schedule, since over-liming causes its own nutrient problems."
