long_description: "If your lawn is struggling despite regular care, the problem is usually underneath it. Soil composition changes constantly through plant growth, leaching, erosion, fertilizer applications, and added organic matter, so what was true three years ago may not be true now. Soil testing is the best way to understand your soil's health and its ability to support healthy plant growth — it reveals fertility status and identifies nutrient deficiencies or imbalances. The test measures nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, and pH, and from those numbers we make tailored recommendations for the amendments your lawn actually needs. It is also the cheapest step in the whole program, because understanding your specific soil prevents applications you did not need in the first place. Proper nutrients let grass grow strong, resist pests and disease, and tolerate environmental stress."

faqs:
  - q: "What does a soil test actually tell you?"
    a: "Your pH plus nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium levels. Together those explain most cases where a lawn is being fed properly and still failing — almost always because acidic soil has locked the nutrients away from the roots."
  - q: "How often should soil be tested?"
    a: "Every two to three years for an established lawn on a maintenance program, and at the start of any new program. Soil chemistry shifts slowly, so annual testing rarely tells you anything new."
