faqs:
  - q: "How do I know if my lawn needs aeration or dethatching?"
    a: "They fix different problems. If water runs off or puddles instead of soaking in and the lawn gives up as soon as summer heat arrives, the ground is compacted and aeration is the answer. If the lawn looks fine briefly then stalls, seed never takes, and the same thin patches return every year, there is usually a dead thatch layer trapping everything above the soil. We assess before recommending — plenty of lawns need neither."
  - q: "When should overseeding be done?"
    a: "Timing and seedbed matter more than the seed. Overseeding works when the ground can actually accept seed, which often means aerating or dethatching first so it reaches soil rather than sitting on a sealed surface. Late summer into early fall is the strongest window in this region — warm soil, cooler air, less weed competition."
  - q: "How quickly will I see a difference?"
    a: "Not the next day, and anyone promising that is selling you something. Aeration changes how the lawn behaves over a season — less runoff, better recovery, fewer stress spots when conditions turn. Overseeding fills thin areas over a growing cycle. Top dressing evens the lawn out gradually. Soil work is slow on purpose, and it is the reason it lasts."
