  Scranton's housing stock skews old, and that shapes the work. A lot of these homes are
  early-twentieth-century two-and-a-half stories with steep pitches, deep eaves, complex
  valleys and full attics, and many are on their third or fourth roof. Steep pitch sheds
  water well; complex valleys and dormers give it more places to get in. Add the
  freeze-thaw cycle this end of the valley runs through every winter and the failure
  points are predictable — valley flashing, chimney and dormer penetrations, and ice
  dams at deep unvented eaves. We are fifteen minutes away and we quote small repairs as
  readily as full replacements, which matters when the honest answer is that one valley
  needs attention rather than the whole roof.
