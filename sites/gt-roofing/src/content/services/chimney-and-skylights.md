  masonry, install or replace the cap, and waterproof the structure so that
  freeze-thaw stops pulling it apart from the inside — water gets into a hairline
  crack, freezes, expands, and widens it a little more every cycle. On skylights, the
  unit itself is rarely the problem; the flashing and the seal around it usually are.
  We install new skylights with proper step flashing and sealing, including
  energy-efficient and venting models, and we re-flash existing ones that have started
  letting water past. Both are jobs where doing the detail correctly is the entire
  difference.

faqs:
  - q: My skylight leaks. Do I need a new one?
    a: >-
      Usually not. Most skylight leaks are a flashing or seal failure at the curb
      rather than a failure of the unit itself, and re-flashing is far cheaper than
      replacement. We will tell you which one you are dealing with before quoting it.
  - q: What does repointing a chimney mean?
    a: >-
      Grinding out failed mortar joints and packing in fresh mortar. Mortar is softer
      than brick by design and weathers first, and in this climate freeze-thaw
      accelerates it. Repointing at the right time is maintenance; leaving it long
      enough turns it into a rebuild.
  - q: Is a chimney cap necessary?
    a: >-
      It keeps rain, snow and animals out of the flue. Without one, water runs directly
      down inside the chimney and works on the masonry from within, where you cannot
      see it happening until something has moved.
