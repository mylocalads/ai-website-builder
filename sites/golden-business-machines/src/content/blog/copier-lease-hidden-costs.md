A copier quote is easy to compare on one number and hard to compare on the rest. The monthly payment goes on the proposal in bold; the parts that decide what you actually spend over five years tend to live in the service schedule. Here is what to read before signing, whoever you sign with.

## 1. The auto-renewal window

Most copier leases renew automatically for another twelve months unless you give written notice inside a specific window — often 60 to 90 days before the term ends. Miss it, and you spend another year at full price on equipment that is already five years old. Ask for the notice window in writing, then put the date in a calendar the day you sign. This is the single most common thing businesses discover too late.

## 2. Cost per page, not cost per month

A low monthly payment paired with a high service rate is the oldest arithmetic in this industry. If the base agreement charges four or five cents a page for black output against a market range closer to a penny to a penny and a half, a business running 3,000 pages a month is paying well over a hundred dollars more than the quote implied — every month, for the length of the term.

Ask for the black rate and the color rate separately, and ask what triggers a rate increase during the term.

## 3. Minimum page counts

Some agreements bill a monthly minimum whether you print it or not. A contract with a 2,000-page minimum charges you for 2,000 pages in the month you printed 800. That is not necessarily unfair — it can be the trade for a lower base payment — but it needs to be a decision you made rather than a clause you discovered.

## 4. Overage multipliers

Where there is an included allowance, there is an overage rate. Pages beyond the allowance often bill at two to three times the base rate. The risk is an allowance set deliberately low so the headline number looks competitive and the overages carry the margin. Compare the allowance against your real metered volume, not against an estimate.

## 5. The costs at each end

Documentation fees at signing typically run $65 to $125. Equipment insurance is often $5 to $15 per machine per month. At the end of the term, return freight is commonly $175 to $225 per machine and is usually the customer's responsibility. None of these are unreasonable on their own. All of them belong in the comparison.

## How to make the comparison fair

Before you look at a single proposal, get your own numbers:

- **Meter every device.** Actual monthly volume by machine, split black and color.
- **Total the current spend.** Lease or depreciation, service, toner, and the staff time spent ordering supplies and chasing repairs.
- **Write down your response requirement.** How long can a department be down before it costs you more than the machine?

Then ask every vendor to quote against those numbers and to show the assumptions on the page.

That is how GBM writes a proposal: the device, the term, the included allowance, the cost per page in black and in color, what happens at renewal, and what happens at the end.
