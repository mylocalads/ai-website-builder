Hurricane season runs June through November, and Orlando does not need a named storm to do roof damage — an ordinary August afternoon will manage it. The work that protects a roof happens months before the forecast, not during it.

## Get it inspected before the season, not after

The single most useful thing you can do is have someone get on the roof while the weather is calm. Most storm failures start at something that was already compromised: a lifted shingle edge, a cracked boot around a vent, sealant that has given up around a skylight. None of those are visible from the ground, and all of them are cheap to fix in April and expensive to fix in September.

An inspection should tell you three things: the condition of the covering, the condition of the flashing and penetrations, and whether the decking underneath is still sound. If a contractor only looks at the shingles, you have half an answer.

## Clear what is going to become a projectile

Trim back limbs that overhang the roofline. Orlando's tree canopy is one of the best things about living here and one of the hardest things on roofs — a branch that touches the covering in normal wind will scour it, and one that breaks in a storm goes straight through.

Clear the gutters too. Central Florida rain arrives faster than most drainage was designed for, and water that cannot leave the roof will find a way in through the fascia instead.

## Know what your roof is rated for

Florida wind-uplift code has tightened considerably over the last two decades. If your roof predates the current standard, the question is not whether it looks fine — it is whether the attachment underneath meets what the structure now needs. That is worth knowing before a storm rather than during a claim.

## Photograph it now

Take dated photographs of your roof, your gutters and your exterior while everything is intact. If you end up filing a claim, the difference between a smooth approval and a denial is very often whether you can demonstrate the condition beforehand. Carriers in Florida deny plenty of legitimate roof claims as wear and tear; documented before-and-after is the cheapest insurance against that.

## After a storm: secure first, decide second

If a storm does open the roof up, the priority is stopping further damage — emergency board-up and tarping — before anyone starts talking about the rebuild. Every hour a structure sits exposed adds water damage, and in Orlando's humidity mold can establish within 24 to 48 hours of moisture getting in.
