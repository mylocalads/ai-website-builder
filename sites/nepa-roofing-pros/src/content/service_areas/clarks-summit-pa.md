Clarks Summit and the Abingtons sit higher and more exposed than the valley floor, and elevation is the whole difference.

Snow lies longer up here than it does in Scranton a few hundred feet below. That means the load sits on the roof for more of the winter, and the melt-and-refreeze cycle at the eaves runs for more of it — which is why ice damming is a more persistent problem in the Abingtons than most homeowners expect when they move up from the valley. The exposure adds wind on top of it, and wind is what finds a shingle that was fastened into the mat rather than through a reinforced nailing zone.

Two things follow. First, the eave and ventilation detailing has to be right, not adequate. Second, this is the part of our territory where metal genuinely earns its price — a standing seam or rib panel roof sheds snow instead of holding it, and it is a long time before you think about it again. We install both, along with Owens Corning Duration Series shingles where a homeowner would rather stay with asphalt, across Clarks Green, Glenburn, Waverly and Chinchilla.
