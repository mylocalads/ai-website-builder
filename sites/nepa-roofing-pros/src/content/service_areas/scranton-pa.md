Scranton is a city of tall, narrow, close-set houses with steep roofs and deep eaves. It is beautiful, and it is unforgiving if the flashing and venting are not right. We work the city daily out of West Pittston, about twenty minutes down the valley.

Much of the housing stock here predates the war, and a lot of it has been reroofed more than once by more than one contractor. What we most often find when we get up there is layers — old shingle left on rather than torn off, flashing reused when it should have been replaced, and attic ventilation that was fine for a 1920s roof assembly and is not fine under modern insulation. That combination is exactly what produces ice dams: warm air reaches the deck, snow melts, the meltwater runs to a cold eave, refreezes, and backs up under the shingles.

Fixing it properly means tearing off to the deck, replacing the flashing rather than reusing it, and correcting the ventilation as part of the job. Steep Scranton roofs also need real staging, which is a labour cost we would rather explain at the estimate than surprise you with.

Green Ridge, the Hill Section, the West Side, South Side, Minooka — we cover all of it, residential and commercial.
