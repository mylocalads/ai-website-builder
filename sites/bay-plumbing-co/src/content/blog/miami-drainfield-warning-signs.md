A lot of Miami-Dade property is still on a septic tank and drainfield rather than city sewer, particularly south and west of the built-up core. Those systems work well for decades and then fail with less warning than most homeowners expect — and they fail faster here than almost anywhere else in the country, because our water table sits so high that a field has very little margin to work with.

Even "small" septic system problems caused by damage to the drainfield can lead to big issues if they are not dealt with right away. Here is what to watch for.

## 1. Ground over the field that is soggy, or suspiciously green

The most common first sign, and the most commonly ignored. If one patch of yard stays wet when the rest has dried, or if the grass over the drainfield is noticeably greener and growing faster than the grass around it, effluent is surfacing instead of percolating. That is the field telling you it has stopped absorbing.

This is not a wait-and-see. Standing effluent is a health issue before it is a plumbing issue.

## 2. Slow drains everywhere, not at one fixture

One slow sink is a sink problem. Every drain in the house running slow at once — tubs, showers, toilets, laundry — is a system problem, and on a septic property that usually points at the tank or the field rather than at any individual line.

## 3. Gurgling

Air being pulled back through the traps as water struggles to leave. If you hear the toilet gurgle when the washing machine empties, the system is not moving water the way it should.

## 4. Sewage odour outside after rain

Heavy rain saturates ground that is already close to capacity. If you get an outdoor smell specifically after a storm, the field is likely already at its limit in dry weather.

## 5. It has been more than five years since anyone pumped the tank

Most residential tanks want attention every three to five years, depending on size and how many people are on the system. A tank that has never been pumped is the cheapest possible thing to check — and while it is open, the drainfield can be inspected properly at the same time.

## What it costs to leave it

A drainfield caught early can sometimes be repaired. A drainfield left until the house stops draining generally gets replaced, which means excavation, and in Miami that often means lifting driveway or paving to reach it.
