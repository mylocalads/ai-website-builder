faqs:
  - q: What is the difference between snaking and jetting?
    a: A cable punches a hole through the blockage so water moves again. Jetting scours the pipe wall, which is what actually removes grease and root intrusion. A line cleared with a cable often backs up again within weeks; a jetted line usually does not.
  - q: Do I need a camera inspection?
    a: If the line has backed up more than once, yes. Repeat stoppages usually mean something structural — a belly, a crack, a root intrusion or a collapsed section. Finding and revealing that concealed damage with video equipment is far cheaper than digging to look.
  - q: What is a clean-out and do I want one?
    a: It is an access point cut into the line at ground level. Where a property has no usable access we will dig down, locate the drain line and add a clean-out T. It turns future service calls from an excavation into a fifteen-minute job.
  - q: Why do Miami houses back up so often?
    a: A lot of the older stock through the Grove, the Gables and South Miami still runs on cast iron laid two generations ago. Cast iron scales and cracks with age, and the high water table lets roots find the cracks. It is the single most common thing we camera.

A stoppage is the job most likely to be done badly, because "the water is moving again" looks identical whether the line was properly cleared or merely punctured. We do it the other way round: find out what is actually in the pipe, clear it so it stays clear, and leave you with access for next time.

Our fleet carries state-of-the-art video equipment, so finding and revealing concealed damage in your sewer lines is no problem for our seasoned Miami-Dade plumbers. If the camera shows grease or root intrusion, jetting scours it out. If it shows a break, a belly or a collapsed section, we can repair or replace the line — and because we own the excavation and concrete-removal equipment, that repair does not get handed to another company.

One customer described the whole sequence: two technicians came out the morning after the call and located the problem; the next morning two more dug down two feet to find the drain line, added a clean-out T, and were done by noon — "cleaned up and left everything perfect."
