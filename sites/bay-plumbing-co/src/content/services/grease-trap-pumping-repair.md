A grease trap is the least interesting piece of equipment in a commercial kitchen right up until the moment it stops working, at which point it becomes the only thing anyone is thinking about. Miami has a lot of restaurants and not many plumbing companies that own the trucks to service them properly.

We handle inspections, repairs and pump-outs for all grease traps and septic tanks. The pumping itself is straightforward; the part that matters commercially is scheduling. Because we own our equipment rather than sub-contracting or renting it, we can put you on an interval that suits your kitchen and turn up before opening or after close instead of in the middle of lunch.

Where grease has already got past the trap and into the line, jetting is the right answer — it scours the pipe wall rather than punching a hole through the blockage, which is why a jetted line stays clear and a cabled one usually does not. We carry video equipment too, so if the line has backed up more than once we can look at what is actually going on in there rather than guessing.

And if the trap itself has failed, we replace it — excavation and concrete removal included, in house, on one invoice.
