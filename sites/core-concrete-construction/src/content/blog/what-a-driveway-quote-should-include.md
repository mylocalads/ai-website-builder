If you collect three quotes for a concrete driveway around Indianapolis, you will very likely get three quite different numbers. That is not because two of the contractors are trying it on. It is usually because they are quoting different work, and the quotes do not say so clearly enough for you to notice.

Here is what should be on the page.

## Demolition and haul-away

If there is an existing slab, someone has to break it up and take it away. That is real cost — labour, a dumpster or truck, and disposal fees. A quote that does not mention removal either includes it silently or does not include it at all, and you find out which on the day.

## Excavation and base preparation

This is the line that separates a driveway that is flat in fifteen years from one that is not. The subgrade should be excavated to the right depth, filled where it needs filling, and mechanically compacted. Ask what depth of stone base is going in and whether it is being compacted. If the answer is vague, that is your answer.

## Thickness and reinforcement

Four inches is standard for residential drives, thicker where heavy vehicles will park or turn. Reinforcement — fibre in the mix, wire mesh, or rebar — should be specified rather than implied. A quote that does not state thickness is not really a quote.

## Joint layout

Control joints should be cut, not tooled in and hoped for, and the spacing should suit the slab thickness. It is a small line item that decides where your driveway cracks for the rest of its life.

## The finish

Broom finish is the standard. Stamped, textured and exposed aggregate cost more because they are more labour and more material. If you are comparing a broom-finish quote against a decorative one, you are not comparing like with like.

## Permits

In Indianapolis a driveway connecting to a city street needs a permit. Hendricks County and the Town of Plainfield have their own requirements for drives and patios. The quote should say who is filing it and whether the fee is included or separate. If a contractor tells you the permit is your job, factor in your own time.

## What is not on the quote but should be discussed

**Access.** How far the concrete has to travel from the truck to the pour changes the labour. A back garden patio with no truck access is a different job from a front driveway.

**Timing.** Most residential driveways are a one-day pour. Ask whether yours is, and ask when the crew arrives.

**Cure time.** You should be told to stay off it for 24 hours and to keep vehicles off for about a week. If nobody mentions it, ask.

**Cleanup.** Ask explicitly what the site looks like when they leave.

## The cheapest quote is usually explained by one of these

Nine times out of ten, a quote that is dramatically lower has skipped the base prep, thinned the slab, left the demolition out, or left the permit to you. Any of those is a legitimate choice if you make it knowingly. The problem is being handed a number and not being told which one it is.
