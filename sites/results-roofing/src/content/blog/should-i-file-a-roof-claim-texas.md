Ask around a DFW neighborhood after a hailstorm and you will hear both
positions argued with equal conviction. Half your neighbors say file
immediately, that is what insurance is for. The other half say filing is how
you get dropped.

Both are describing something real. Here is the trade-off as clearly as we can
lay it out — with the caveat that we are roofers, not your insurance agent, and
the specifics of your policy belong to them.

## What has changed in this market

Three things, all of them recent enough that advice from five years ago no
longer applies cleanly.

**Deductibles have moved.** Percentage-based wind and hail deductibles are now
standard across North Texas. Homeowners who held a 1% deductible are being moved
to 2% at renewal, and some are seeing 3%. The old fixed-dollar roof deductibles
of $500 or $1,000 have largely disappeared and are not coming back.

**Carriers are underwriting roof age aggressively.** Older roofs are drawing
non-renewals and ACV-only endorsements. Some policies now decline roof coverage
entirely past a certain age.

**Premiums are rising regardless.** This is the part that reframes the whole
question. Rates in hail-exposed ZIP codes have climbed for homeowners who have
never filed a claim, because pricing responds to regional loss experience rather
than only to your individual history.

## The catch-22 nobody explains

Put those together and a genuine bind appears.

Do not file, and your roof ages. Each year it accumulates damage nobody has
documented. Eventually it reaches the age where your carrier either declines
roof coverage, moves you to actual cash value only, or non-renews. At that
point a replacement is entirely yours to fund.

File repeatedly, and you carry a claims history that can affect renewal.

There is no clean escape from this. What there is, is a better-informed
decision — which starts with knowing whether you actually have damage.

## Get the inspection regardless of what you decide

An inspection is not a claim. It costs nothing and commits you to nothing, and
it produces something valuable either way: a dated, photographed record of your
roof's condition.

If there is significant damage, you now have documentation with a clear date of
loss — which is exactly what a carrier will scrutinize if you file.

If there is not, you have a baseline. The next time a storm rolls through, the
difference between your roof before and after is demonstrable rather than
arguable. That record is also what makes it hard for a carrier to later
characterize damage as pre-existing.

## Questions worth asking your agent

Before you decide, five minutes on the phone with your agent is worth more than
any article:

- Is my roof covered at replacement cost or actual cash value, and does that
  change with roof age?
- What is my wind and hail deductible in dollars, not percent?
- Is there a premium discount for Class 4 impact-resistant shingles, and how
  much?
- What is my policy's window for reporting a loss?
- How does a single weather claim affect my renewal, specifically?

That third question surprises people. Impact-resistant shingles cost more up
front, and several major Texas carriers discount for them — enough that
homeowners have reported the discount changing the math on the upgrade
entirely. If you are replacing the roof anyway, it is worth pricing both.
