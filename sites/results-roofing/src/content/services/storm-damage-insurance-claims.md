faqs:
  - q: What are the common signs of storm damage I should look for?
    a: Cracked or missing shingles, dents on metal surfaces such as vents and gutters, water stains on ceilings, and granule buildup in gutters. From the roof itself, look for damage with no discernible pattern, black hail hits, dents that feel soft or bruised to the touch, and debris underneath multiple shingles.
  - q: What happens if I ignore hail damage?
    a: Neglecting hail damage leads to compromised structural integrity, leaks, and further deterioration — which usually means a far larger bill later. It can also close your window to file a claim at all, and carriers increasingly decline coverage on roofs with unrepaired damage.
  - q: How long do I have to file?
    a: Texas homeowners policies generally require prompt notice of a loss, and many set an outside limit of roughly a year from the date of the storm — but the exact window is set by your policy, so read yours or ask your agent. The practical risk is that the longer you wait, the easier it is for the carrier to argue the damage is old or pre-existing.
  - q: Do you actually attend the adjuster meeting?
    a: Yes. Our inspector is on the roof while your adjuster is, so anything missed gets raised on the spot instead of disputed weeks later. If the approved scope still comes back short, we file a supplement with the photo documentation to support it.
