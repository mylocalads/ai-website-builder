There is a question almost nobody asks when they are getting roofing quotes, and it
is the one that decides most of what happens afterwards:

**Who, specifically, is going to be on my roof?**

Not which company's name is on the estimate. Who is on the ladder.

## How it usually works

Across most of this industry, roofing companies sell work and then place it with
whichever subcontracted crew has capacity that week. The salesperson who measured
your roof does not know the crew. The crew has never met the person who wrote
your warranty. And the crew is paid by the job, which means the incentive is to
be finished, not to be thorough.

Most of the time you never find out, because most roofs look fine on the day they
are completed. A roof that was rushed and a roof that was done properly are
visually identical from the driveway. The difference shows up in year four, at
the flashing, after a freeze-thaw winter.

## Why it matters for your warranty

A workmanship warranty is a promise about installation — that the underlayment,
the ice-and-water membrane at the eaves, the valley detail and the nailing
pattern were all done correctly.

Ask yourself who honours that promise when the installer was a subcontractor who
has since moved on, or whose company has dissolved and reformed under a new name.
In practice, the homeowner ends up in the middle of a conversation between two
businesses, each of which has a reason to say it was the other one.

That is why we do not subcontract. Every installer at MNM is on our payroll and
was personally trained by Miguel, who is on site with them. When we put a 10-year
workmanship warranty in writing on a full replacement — plus our 15-year craftsman
warranty — there is nobody else in the chain to point at.

## Four questions worth asking every roofer you call

1. **"Is the crew your own employees, or subcontracted?"** Ask it directly. The
   answer, and how comfortable they are giving it, tells you a lot.
2. **"Who will be on site the day of the install, and will anyone from your
   office be there?"** A company that cannot name anyone is telling you something.
3. **"What exactly does your workmanship warranty cover, and who honours it?"**
   Get the number of years and the scope in writing, not in conversation.
4. **"What happens if you find rotten decking under the old roof?"** Every honest
   answer here involves replacing it and pricing it per sheet. Any answer that
   involves going over it is a reason to stop.

## The Northeast Pennsylvania part

None of this is theoretical here. Roofs in Pike, Wayne, Monroe, Lackawanna,
Luzerne and Lehigh counties spend the winter under a freeze-thaw cycle that works
at every seam, and ice dams push meltwater back up under the shingles at the
eaves. A roof that was installed correctly shrugs at that. A roof that was
installed quickly starts leaking somewhere you cannot see, into decking you will
not inspect, for two or three years before it reaches a ceiling.

The install is the product. The shingle is just what it is made of.
