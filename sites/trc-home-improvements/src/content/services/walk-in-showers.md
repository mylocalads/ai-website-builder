faqs:
  - q: Is a door-less shower going to spray water everywhere?
    a: Not when it is laid out correctly. The panel length, pan slope and fixture position are all sized to keep water inside — that geometry is the design work, and it is why a door-less shower needs planning rather than just leaving the door off.
  - q: Can a walk-in shower work in a small bathroom?
    a: Usually yes, and it often makes the room feel bigger. Losing the tub apron and the swing of a shower door recovers real floor area, and a single glass panel keeps sightlines open across the whole space.
  - q: What makes a walk-in shower safer?
    a: No threshold to step over, grab bars anchored into wall blocking, a bench to sit on, a handheld fixture you can reach seated, and pan tile chosen for slip resistance. Those are decisions made at design stage, not accessories bolted on later.
  - q: How is the floor drain handled on a curbless pan?
    a: The pan is sloped toward the drain and the surrounding floor is stepped or recessed so water never runs out of the shower zone. Where it works, we hide the drain in the mosaic pattern so it disappears into the floor.
