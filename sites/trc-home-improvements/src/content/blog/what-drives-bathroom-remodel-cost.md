Nobody publishes a price for a bathroom because nobody honestly can. What I can do is tell you exactly which levers move the number, so you can walk into a consultation knowing where your money is going.

## The variables that matter most

**Whether plumbing moves.** This is the single biggest swing. Keeping the drain and valve where they are is straightforward. Moving a drain across a joist bay, or relocating a valve to a different wall, means opening the floor or wall and getting it inspected. It is not exotic work, but it is real work.

**Pan style.** A pre-formed base drops in. A fully tiled pan is built by hand — mud bed, slope, membrane, then mosaic — and takes days rather than hours. Both are correct choices; they just are not the same price.

**Tile size and material.** Large-format porcelain needs a flatter substrate than small tile, so the prep work goes up before a single piece is set. Natural stone needs sealing and different handling than porcelain. Mosaic pans take far longer to lay than a sheet base.

**Glass.** A fixed panel is one price. A frameless enclosure with a hinged door and custom-cut glass is a different one, and it is usually the line item that surprises people most.

**Scope creep from what we find.** Behind a thirty-year-old surround there is sometimes rot, sometimes a hidden leak, sometimes framing that was never right. A good quote flags that risk before demo rather than discovering it and handing you a change order.

## Why the cheapest bid is usually the most expensive job

I am blunt about this because I have made a real part of my living fixing it.

The cheapest price almost never has value. It has to come from somewhere, and it does not come from a contractor deciding to earn less — it comes out of waterproofing, out of substrate prep, out of the time it takes to set tile properly. Those are the parts you cannot inspect once the job is finished, which is precisely why they are where corners get cut. When it fails in three years, you pay twice: once for the tear-out, once for the rebuild.

The other end is not automatically value either. A very high number often just means a large company subbing the work out to a crew you will never meet, with the margin paying for the office rather than the craftsmanship.

The middle ground is generally where price and value actually meet. That is where we sit, deliberately.

## What to ask any contractor you are considering

- What waterproofing system are you using, and does it go behind the whole wet wall?
- Who is physically on my job every day — you, an employee, or a sub?
- Is the tile you are proposing rated for a wet space?
- What happens to the schedule and the price if you open the wall and find rot?
- Can I see a shower you built five years ago, not just five weeks ago?

The answers will tell you more than the number at the bottom of the page.
