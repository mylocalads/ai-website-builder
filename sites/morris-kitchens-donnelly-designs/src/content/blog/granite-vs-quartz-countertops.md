Countertops set the tone of the whole room — material, colour, veining, and edge
profile do more visual work than almost anything else in a kitchen. They are
also where most people stall, because the two front-runners look similar in a
photograph and behave nothing alike in a house.

## Granite

Granite is natural stone, so no two slabs are the same. It is one of the hardest
materials on earth, which makes it extremely resistant to scratches, heat, and
stains. The trade-off is that it is porous: it needs resealing roughly once a
year to keep looking the way it did on day one.

Choose granite if you want a surface that is genuinely one of a kind and you do
not mind a small annual maintenance ritual.

## Quartz

Quartz is engineered, which means consistent colour and pattern from slab to
slab, and — more importantly — it is nonporous. It stands up to moisture,
spills, splatters, heat, impact, and sharp objects with essentially no
maintenance, which is why it wins in busy households with young children.

Choose quartz if you want it to look the same in ten years without thinking
about it.

## The other four

Marble and natural wood have a beauty nothing else matches, and both ask for a
real cleaning and maintenance routine in return — be honest with yourself about
whether you will keep it up. Solid surface like Corian is seamless and
repairable. Laminate lines such as Wilsonart and Formica have improved
enormously and remain the right call when the budget needs to go into the
cabinetry instead.

## Ask yourself four questions

- What is my preferred style?
- How much can I spend?
- What is my lifestyle — do I entertain, bake, have small children?
- Am I willing to follow a specialised cleaning and maintenance routine?

The perfect countertop is the one that balances your style against your
functional needs. If you love to entertain, look for the marriage of style and
convenience. If you are an avid cook or baker, choose something hard-working
that will not stand in the way.
