long_description: Back pain is the most common reason patients come to Comprehensive Pain Specialists, and the most commonly mistreated. Our providers begin by identifying the actual pain generator — a disc, a facet joint, the sacroiliac joint, or a nerve root — rather than treating the symptom in the dark. Treatment then draws on epidural steroid injections, facet joint injections, medial branch blocks, radiofrequency ablation, spinal cord stimulation, and the Vertiflex and MILD procedures for spinal stenosis. The goal is to reduce pain and restore function without an operation you do not need.

faqs:
  - q: Do I need an MRI before my first appointment?
    a: Bring whatever imaging you already have, but you do not need new imaging to be seen. Part of the first visit is determining what imaging, if any, would actually change the plan.
  - q: I already had back surgery and the pain came back. Can you help?
    a: Yes. Failed back surgery syndrome is one of the conditions we treat most often, and it is treated as its own diagnosis rather than as a complication to be managed. Spinal cord stimulation is one of several options for post-surgical back pain.
  - q: Will I be put on opioids?
    a: Not by default. This is an interventional practice — the plan is built around procedures and targeted treatments, with medication as one tool among many rather than the whole approach.
