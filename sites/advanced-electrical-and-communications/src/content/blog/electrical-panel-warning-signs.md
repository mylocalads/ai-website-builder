Most homeowners don't think about their electrical panel until something trips. That's normal — a panel is designed to sit quietly in a basement or utility closet and do its job without being noticed. But a few warning signs are worth more than a quick reset, especially in a lot of the older housing stock we see across Dupont, Wilkes-Barre, Scranton and the rest of Northeastern Pennsylvania.

## 1. A breaker trips more than once

A breaker tripping occasionally, tied to a clear cause — you ran the space heater and the microwave at once — usually isn't cause for alarm. A breaker that trips repeatedly, or trips with nothing unusual plugged in, is telling you something about either the circuit or the breaker itself. Resetting it every time treats the symptom, not the cause.

## 2. The panel or a breaker feels warm

A breaker panel should be room temperature to the touch. Warmth at the panel, at a specific breaker, or at an outlet on that circuit points to a loose connection or a connection carrying more current than it's rated for — both of which get worse, not better, if left alone.

## 3. Lights flicker or dim when an appliance kicks on

Some dimming when a large appliance starts up is normal. Flickering that's noticeable across multiple rooms, or that happens with smaller loads, can point to a loose connection somewhere between the panel and the fixture — sometimes at the panel itself.

## 4. The panel still uses fuses, or is a known problem brand

A lot of homes in our area were built or last rewired decades ago, and some panels from that era — certain fuse boxes and a handful of specific breaker panel brands — have known reliability issues that modern panels don't share. If you're not sure what's in your panel, that alone is worth a look.

## 5. You're adding a major appliance, an EV charger, or a home addition

Panels are sized for the electrical load they were installed to handle. Adding a large new load — a hot tub, an EV charger, a home addition — is one of the most common reasons a panel that's been fine for years suddenly isn't sized correctly anymore.
