  Scranton's housing stock is old, dense, and mostly steep-pitched — a lot of two-and-a-half
  story frame homes from the coal era with narrow side yards and roofs that have already been
  covered more than once. Two-layer tear-offs are common here, and so is decking that has
  quietly taken on water at a valley for a decade. We have replaced roofs across the city and
  the pattern holds: what looks like a shingle problem from the street is usually a flashing or
  a ventilation problem once you are on the ladder. One recent Green Ridge-area job was a
  30-square two-layer tear-off finished in two days, in Owens Corning TruDefinition Duration,
  Slatestone Gray. The homeowner's note afterward was about the cleanup — the crew left the job
  site as clean or cleaner than they found it, which in a city with front yards this small is
  the difference between a good week and a bad one for the whole block.
