faqs:
  - q: What makes seamless gutters better than sectional ones?
    a: >-
      Seams are where gutters fail. Sectional gutters are joined every ten feet or so, and every
      one of those joints is a place for sealant to age out and water to find its way through.
      Seamless runs are formed on site in one continuous length per elevation, so the only
      joints on the whole system are at corners and outlets. They also look considerably better
      from the ground.
  - q: Do I really need to replace my gutters when I replace the roof?
    a: >-
      Not always, but it is worth deciding at the same time rather than after. Gutters have to
      come off or get worked around during a tear-off, and old aluminum that has been up
      through fifteen NEPA winters often does not go back on cleanly. If yours have life left,
      we will say so; if they are dented, sagging, or pulling away from the fascia, doing them
      alongside the roof saves a second mobilization.
  - q: What causes ice damming, and will new gutters fix it?
    a: >-
      Ice dams come from heat escaping into the attic, melting snow on the upper roof, and that
      meltwater refreezing at the cold eave. Gutters do not cause it and new gutters alone will
      not cure it — attic insulation and ventilation are the real levers. What good gutters and
      properly placed downspouts do is move water away from the house fast once it is running,
      which limits the damage while you deal with the underlying cause.
  - q: Can you match the color to my roof and siding?
    a: >-
      Yes. The aluminum coil comes in dozens of colors, so we can pick a run that disappears
      against the fascia or one that deliberately picks up a trim color. It is one of the
      choices we go through during the in-home estimate rather than leaving to the crew on
      install day.
