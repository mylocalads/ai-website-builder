A storm rolls through Lackawanna County, and by the next afternoon there are trucks in the neighborhood and cards in the door. Most homeowners we meet at that point have already been told three different things by three different people, and none of them wrote anything down.

The order you do things in that first week matters more than most people expect. Here is how we would run it.

## Day one: look, but from the ground

Do not get on the roof. Wet or damaged shingles are how people end up in the emergency room, and there is very little you can learn up there that a contractor with a ladder cannot learn for you.

What you can do from the ground is useful:

- Walk the perimeter and photograph anything on the lawn — shingle pieces, granule piles at the downspout outlets, torn flashing, limbs.
- Check the soft metal. Hail leaves dents on gutters, downspouts, window screens, AC condenser fins and mailboxes long before you can see anything on the shingles. That collateral evidence is often what establishes the storm was severe enough to matter.
- Look at your ceilings, upstairs first. A stain that was not there yesterday is a leak that is already running.
- Write down the date. Insurers work from a date of loss, and "sometime in July" is a much weaker claim than a specific storm.

## Day one, still: stop active water

If water is actively coming in, that is the exception to everything above — it needs to be addressed now, not after the paperwork. Call someone who will come out. We are reachable around the clock at (570) 382-8629 for exactly this reason.

Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, so a temporary repair is not something you have to seek permission for. Keep the receipt.

## Day two: get an inspection before you file

This is the step people skip, and it is the one that changes outcomes.

Filing a claim on a roof that turns out to have no storm damage is not free — it goes on your record with the carrier either way. Having someone qualified look first tells you whether there is a claim worth filing at all. Our estimate is free and carries no obligation, and if the honest answer is that your roof has years left in it, that is the answer you will get.

If there is damage, you now go to your insurer with a photo record and a clear date of loss instead of a description.

## The rest of the week: be careful who you sign with

The pattern homeowners describe on every roofing forum after a storm is the same, and it is worth naming plainly:

- A crew from well outside the area works the neighborhood door to door.
- They promise a "free roof" and press for a signature the same day.
- They ask for the first insurance check to be endorsed over to them.

Some of those companies do fine work. Many of them are gone by the time a warranty question comes up. Two questions sort it out quickly: where is your local address, and what is your state license number? Ours are 1 Keystone Place in Jessup, PA HIC# 017000 and MD HIC# 161389.

One more thing worth knowing: if a contractor offers to cover or waive your deductible, that is a red flag rather than a favor. You are the one whose name is on the claim.
