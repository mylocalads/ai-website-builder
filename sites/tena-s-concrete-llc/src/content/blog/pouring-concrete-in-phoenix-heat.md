If you've ever asked a concrete crew why they show up before sunrise in the summer, the answer isn't just about beating traffic. It's about the concrete itself.

## Heat speeds up the clock

Concrete cures through a chemical reaction — not by drying out — and that reaction speeds up as temperatures rise. In the Phoenix area, surface temperatures on a driveway or patio slab can climb well past the air temperature once the sun is on it directly. A pour that would have a comfortable working window in cooler weather can start setting up before it's been properly finished.

That matters because a rushed finish is a weak finish. Trowel work, edging, and any stamped or textured pattern all have to happen inside that window. Miss it and the result can be a rougher surface, inconsistent color, or a finish that's more prone to surface cracking down the line.

## Why we start as early as 5:00am

We're on site as early as 5:00am, Monday through Saturday, specifically so forming, pouring, and finishing happen while temperatures are at their lowest for the day. It gives us a longer, more controllable window to do the work right rather than racing the sun.

## What this means for your project

- **Driveways and sidewalks** get a smoother, more consistent finish when the crew isn't fighting a fast-setting mix.
- **Stamped patios** need extra working time for pattern and color work — an early start protects that window.
- **Curing** still needs to happen properly after we leave, which is why we talk through care instructions with every client before we wrap up.
