Every serious wind or hail event in the Wyoming Valley is followed by the same thing: unfamiliar trucks, clipboard in hand, offering a free inspection. Some of those crews are legitimate. Many are not, and the ones that are not follow a recognizable pattern.

## 1. They found you, you did not find them

A roofer working off referrals and reviews does not need to canvass a neighborhood. Door-knocking after a storm is a volume business, and volume businesses need to close on the first visit.

## 2. There is no local address

Ask where the shop is. Not the sales office, not the P.O. box — the shop. Ours is at 20 W Grove St in Edwardsville, and it has been in the Wyoming Valley for fifteen years. A company that cannot point at a building nearby is a company you cannot find in eighteen months when a shingle lifts.

## 3. The urgency is theirs, not yours

"We're only in the area this week" is a scheduling constraint that belongs to the contractor, not the roof. Genuine urgency sounds different: water is actively entering the house, or a limb has opened the envelope. That is worth moving on today. A sales window is not.

## 4. They want the insurance check before the scope is settled

The claim process has an order to it. Damage gets documented, an adjuster inspects, a scope gets approved, and then work is priced against that scope. A contractor who wants to talk about signing over the check before an adjuster has been on the roof has skipped the part where anyone establishes what is actually wrong.

## 5. There is no line-item estimate

Ask for tear-off, underlayment, ventilation, and disposal broken out separately. A one-line number is impossible to compare against another bid, which is usually the point. Every estimate we write itemizes, because we would rather you compare us honestly than take our word for it.

## 6. The warranty has no name behind it

A warranty is only worth the company standing behind it. A lifetime manufacturer warranty on materials is real and transferable. A workmanship warranty is only as good as whether the business exists to honor it — which is why ours is ten years and why we are careful to say who "we" is. When you call us about your roof, we are the roofers. We do not subcontract out.
