Drive through Green Ridge in Scranton, or the older streets of Kingston and Wilkes-Barre, and you are looking at slate roofs installed before the Second World War that are still doing their job. Slate lasts a century or more. Asphalt lasts twenty years. And yet the most common quote a homeowner with a slate roof receives is a tear-off and shingle replacement.

There is a reason for that, and it is not usually about the roof.

## Slate is a skill, not a material

Working slate means knowing how to cut and set individual slates, how to handle the copper and flashing details around valleys and chimneys, and how to move on the roof without breaking three slates for every one you replace. Most crews in this area do not have that skill, so the honest version of the quote is "we cannot repair this," and the quote you receive instead is for the work they can do.

We have installed hundreds of slate roofs in this area. That is why our answer is different.

## When repair is the right call

- **Scattered broken or slipped slates.** Individual slates can be cut and replaced. The surrounding roof is unaffected.
- **Failed flashing at valleys, chimneys or dormers.** Flashing is frequently the actual source of a leak that gets blamed on the slate. Replacing flashing is far cheaper than replacing a roof.
- **Nail fatigue in isolated sections.** Fasteners fail before slate does. Sections can be reset.
- **The slate itself is sound.** Tap it. Good slate rings; delaminating slate sounds dull and flakes at the edges. A roof where most slates still ring is a roof with decades left.

## When replacement is honestly the answer

- **Widespread delamination.** When most of the field is flaking and soft, you are past patching.
- **Deck or structural failure underneath.** If the sheathing has rotted or the framing has moved, the slate has to come off regardless of its condition.
- **A previous repair did real damage.** Slate that has been walked on carelessly, or patched with the wrong fasteners and roofing cement, sometimes cannot be recovered.
