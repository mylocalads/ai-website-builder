Homeowners routinely get quotes ranging ten to twenty thousand dollars on what looks like the same roof, and nobody explains the gap. Sometimes the spread is a padded bid. Sometimes the cheap bid quietly leaves something out. Here is what actually moves the number, so you can tell which is which.

## 1. Roofing squares

A square is 100 square feet of roof surface. It is the unit everything else gets priced against, and it is not the same as your home's square footage — a 1,900 square foot ranch with a simple gable has far fewer squares than a 1,900 square foot house with dormers, valleys and a wraparound porch.

## 2. Pitch

Steep roofs cost more because they are slower and require more safety equipment. Wyoming Valley housing skews steep. If one bid is dramatically lower than the others, check whether it was measured or estimated from the ground.

## 3. What is under the old shingles

Nobody knows the condition of the decking until the tear-off starts. A bid that says nothing about deck replacement is a bid that will produce a change order. Ask every contractor what they charge per sheet of replacement sheathing and get it in writing before work begins — we put it on the estimate up front.

## 4. Material

This is the biggest lever:

- **3-tab asphalt shingle** — the most affordable option, 15 to 20 year life.
- **Architectural asphalt** — 20 to 25 years, better wind performance, modest step up in cost.
- **Metal** — more expensive to install, but 50 years or better, the best fire rating of any roofing material, and lighter than asphalt so it puts less load on the structure.
- **Slate** — the premium option, and the one most Northeastern Pennsylvania pre-war homes already have. Often worth repairing rather than replacing.
- **Flat and rubber (EPDM/TPO)** — for low-slope sections and commercial applications. TPO properly installed and maintained can run 30 years.

## 5. Insurance versus retail

If wind, hail or a tree caused damage across enough of the roof, your homeowner's policy may cover most of the replacement minus your deductible. That is a completely different financial picture from a retail replacement, and it depends on getting the scope right with the adjuster. We attend the adjuster appointment so the approved scope reflects the damage we documented — the correct number of squares, the correct materials, and the related work like gutters and soffits.

## How to compare bids honestly

Ask every contractor for the same four line items broken out: tear-off, underlayment, ventilation, and disposal. Ask what happens if the deck is bad. Ask who is physically doing the work — if the answer is a subcontractor, the workmanship warranty is only as strong as the relationship between two companies you are not part of.
