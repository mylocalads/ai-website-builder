The system is only half the job. The large majority of coating failures in industrial environments trace back to substrate preparation rather than to the coating itself — no proper mechanical profile, or a moisture or contamination problem that was never tested for. We scope profiling, moisture assessment and contamination removal as their own line rather than absorbing them into an install rate, because that is the step that decides whether the floor is still bonded in three years."

faqs:
  - q: "Our last epoxy floor peeled within a year. Why?"
    a: "Almost always preparation, not product. Over 80% of industrial coating failures are attributed to improper substrate preparation — typically no vacuum diamond grind to open the profile, or an untested moisture or vapour problem pushing the coating off from below."
  - q: "How do you know the slab is dry enough to coat?"
    a: "We assess moisture as part of the survey rather than assuming. If the slab is not ready, the honest answer is a moisture-mitigating system or a different sequence, not a coating that will delaminate."
  - q: "Which system do we need?"
    a: "It depends on the exposure. Epoxy for general high-strength seamless duty, polyaspartic and resin where a fast return to service matters, chemical-resistant systems for oils, solvents and acids, abrasion-resistant where forklift wear dominates, and textured slip-resistant finishes in wet or high-traffic pedestrian zones."
  - q: "Can you coat around live operations?"
    a: "Yes — bay by bay, or into a shutdown window. The return-to-service time for the specific system is stated in the written scope before the work is booked."
