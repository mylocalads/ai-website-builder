The commercial roofing complaints that end up in a lawyer's office almost all
follow the same shape. A roof was installed. It leaked somewhere between year
three and year twelve. The owner reached for the warranty and found out it did
not cover what they assumed it covered — or that the company who signed it no
longer exists.

Every one of those outcomes is visible before you sign, if you ask.

## 1. Is this a material warranty or a system warranty?

These are not the same thing, and the difference is where most owners get
caught. A material warranty covers the membrane against manufacturing defect.
It does not cover the installation. If the leak is at a flashing that was
detailed wrong, a material warranty will not help you.

Ask for the term **and** the scope in writing, and ask specifically what
happens if the failure is workmanship.

## 2. Is the contractor authorized by the manufacturer?

Extended system warranties are generally only issuable by contractors the
manufacturer has authorized and trained. If your contractor is not authorized
for the system they are proposing, the warranty you are being promised may not
be a warranty that can actually be issued.

## 3. Have they installed this specific system before?

A contractor who has done shingle work for twenty years has not necessarily
installed a single-ply membrane roof. Low-slope work is a different trade with
different failure modes. Ask how many of this system they have installed, on
what kind of buildings, and whether you can go look at one.

## 4. Who is doing the sheet metal?

On a flat roof, the perimeter and penetration detail is where most failures
start. If the sheet metal is being subbed out to whoever is available, nobody
owns that interface. Ask whether the contractor fabricates their own.

## 5. Will they be here in year twelve?

This is the uncomfortable one, and it is the most important. A warranty is only
worth the continued existence of the company that signed it. Storm-chasers and
out-of-area outfits are gone before the failures appear, and there is nothing
to call.

Ask how long they have been in this market, from where, and who answers the
phone on an emergency.
