If you are collecting roofing quotes and one of them is noticeably cheaper than the rest, there is a good chance it is a lay-over: new shingles installed directly on top of the old ones, no tear-off. It is legal in most cases and it is faster. It is also the single easiest way for a contractor to hand you a problem you will not discover for years.

## What a tear-off shows you

The decking. That is the whole point.

When we strip a roof to bare wood, we walk it. What turns up on older houses around here is fairly predictable: boards gone soft where a valley has been leaking slowly for a decade, rot fanning out around the chimney where the flashing gave up, the occasional split rafter, and delaminated plywood that has been soaking up moisture through a failed vent boot. None of that is visible from a ladder. All of it is visible ten seconds after the old shingles come off.

On a lay-over, none of it is found. The new roof goes down on top of the rot and the rot keeps going.

## Why the new roof is worse too

Set aside what is hidden — the lay-over shortens the life of the shingles you just paid for.

Shingles want a flat plane. Laid over an existing roof they follow every ridge, cup and curl in the old layer beneath, so they never seal down evenly, which is exactly the condition wind lifts. Two layers also trap heat against the deck, and heat is what ages asphalt. Manufacturers know this, which is why warranty terms on layered installations are frequently reduced or void — worth reading before you sign anything.

Then there is weight. Roof structures are engineered for a roof. A second one adds a few hundred pounds per hundred square feet that nobody designed for.

## What it costs to do it properly

More on day one. Tear-off is labour and it is dumpster fees, and any honest contractor will tell you the layover is cheaper up front — that is why it gets quoted.

Across the life of the roof it is the other way round. A torn-off, properly decked, properly membraned roof runs its full rated life. A layover buys you a shorter-lived roof sitting on top of damage that is still spreading, and the day it does have to come off, you are paying to remove two roofs instead of one.
