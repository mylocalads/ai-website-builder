Homeowners ask us for our license number fairly often. It is a reasonable question with an awkward answer: Pennsylvania does not issue a roofing license, so nobody in this state has one.

What exists instead is registration under the Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act — HICPA — with the Attorney General's office. Every contractor doing more than a threshold amount of home improvement work in the state is required to register, and the registration number belongs on their contracts and their advertising. It is worth confirming a contractor has one.

Just be clear about what it proves. HICPA registration confirms that the registrant carries liability insurance and identifies who they are. It certifies nothing whatsoever about the quality of their work, because the state does not test for that. It is a filing, not a qualification. The public lookup database has also spent extended stretches offline, which does not help.

So the licence question, which feels like the responsible thing to ask, does less work here than it would in most states. Here is what does.

## Ask for the estimate in writing

Not a number over the phone, and not a figure called out from the driveway. A written estimate that names the material, states whether the price includes tear-off, and says what happens if rotted decking turns up — a rate per sheet, agreed in advance, rather than an open-ended surprise halfway through.

If getting that written estimate takes three phone calls and a week, you have learned something useful about how the job itself will go.

## Ask about the deposit before you like the price

This is where homeowners get hurt. Large deposits taken up front, then delays, then a contractor who stops answering — it is the most common bad-roofer story on the internet and it is common because it works.

Ask what the deposit is, ask what it is for, and have the payment schedule written into the estimate. Any reluctance to put a payment schedule in writing tells you everything you need to know, and it is a cheap test to run.

## Ask who is actually doing the work

Whether the crew on your roof is employed by the company you hired or subcontracted out. Neither answer is automatically wrong, but you should know, because it determines who you call in two years and whether the workmanship warranty means anything.

## Ask the neighbours

In the absence of a licensing regime, this is the real credential — and it is the one thing an out-of-town crew working the neighbourhood after a storm cannot produce. A local contractor has jobs on nearby streets you can look at, customers who will pick up the phone, and a name that costs them something if they burn it.

We would rather be judged that way. We have 74 Google reviews averaging 4.6, and one customer who has now had us out for a third and fourth roof over five years — his own house, then his son's. That is not a badge we bought. It is just the record.

## Ask about cleanup

Ask, specifically, and listen to how detailed the answer is. Nails left in the lawn is the most common complaint homeowners have about roofers full stop. Someone who has thought about it will tell you about the dumpster placement, the magnet, and who walks the property last.
