faqs:
  - q: There is a stain on my ceiling but the roof looks fine. Where do I start?
    a: With someone getting on the roof and into the attic. Water travels along rafters and decking before it drops, so the stain is rarely under the hole. On Scranton houses the usual suspects, roughly in order, are chimney and sidewall flashing, valleys, vent boots, and ice-dam backup at the eaves. You get photos of whatever we find.
  - q: What is an ice dam and is it my roof's fault?
    a: Not usually. Heat escaping into the attic melts snow off the middle of the roof, the meltwater runs down to the cold overhang, refreezes there, and builds a ridge of ice that backs water up under the shingles — where no shingle was ever designed to stop it. It is as much an insulation and ventilation problem as a roofing one. What roofing can do is put ice-and-water membrane at the eaves so the backup has nowhere to get in, which is why we install it as standard.
  - q: Do you work in winter?
    a: Yes, and it is a real part of what we do. We are reachable seven days a week through the cold months.
  - q: Will you tell me if I actually need a full replacement?
    a: Yes, in both directions. Plenty of roofs we look at have years left and need one detail fixed. A roof with granules gone, brittle curling shingles and leaks in more than one plane is at the end, and repairs stop being economical. Either way you get the reasoning and the photos, and the decision stays yours.
