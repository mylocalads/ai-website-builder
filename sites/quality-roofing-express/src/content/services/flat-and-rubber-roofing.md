faqs:
  - q: Which membrane should I use?
    a: EPDM is the black rubber most people picture — flexible in cold, forgiving to install, long-lived, and the usual choice for a residential back addition. TPO is a welded white sheet that reflects heat, which matters on a commercial building with conditioned space beneath. Torch-down is heat-welded modified bitumen, tough underfoot and well suited to roofs that see traffic. Peel-and-stick goes down where an open flame is a bad idea. We will tell you which fits your roof and why.
  - q: Why does my flat roof keep leaking in the same spot?
    a: Almost always a termination or a seam, not the field of the membrane. Where the flat roof meets a wall, a pitched roof, a drain or a parapet is where water gets in, and it is the part a cheap repair skips because doing it right means opening the detail back up. We trace it to the actual entry point rather than flooding the area with sealant.
  - q: Do you do commercial buildings?
    a: Yes. Commercial and residential both. Small commercial low-slope roofs are a large share of the flat work we do.
  - q: Can a flat roof be repaired, or does it all have to come off?
    a: Depends what failed. A punctured or split seam on an otherwise sound membrane is a repair. A membrane that has gone brittle across the whole field, or a roof holding standing water because the deck has sagged, is a replacement — and patching it just delays that at extra cost. We will show you which one you have.
