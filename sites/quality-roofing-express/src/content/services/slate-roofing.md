Slate is the longest-lived roof there is and the least forgiving to work on — the material is brittle underfoot, the fasteners and flashings are what usually fail rather than the stone itself, and a repair done by someone unfamiliar with it tends to crack four tiles for every one it replaces. We install slate and we work on the slate that is already there. We will also tell you honestly when a slate-look architectural shingle is the better answer, which it sometimes is, because the structure has to carry the weight, and on a house that will change hands in a few years the economics rarely work out.

faqs:
  - q: Should I repair my slate roof or replace it with something else?
    a: If the slate itself is sound and the failures are at the flashings and fasteners, repair — good slate outlives everything else on the house and replacing it with asphalt is a downgrade you cannot undo. If a large share of the tiles are delaminating and shedding, the maths changes. We will look and tell you which situation you are in.
  - q: What is a slate-look shingle?
    a: A laminated architectural asphalt shingle cut and coloured to read as a slate course from the street. It costs a fraction of real slate, weighs a fraction as much, and installs like any other shingle. It is the right answer for homeowners who want the character without the structural and financial commitment — and it is a compromise, which we would rather say out loud.
  - q: Can you match my existing slate?
    a: Often, for repairs. Colour and thickness vary by quarry and by era, so an exact match on a hundred-year-old roof is not always possible. Where it is not, we place matched tiles on the visible planes and use the closest available elsewhere.
