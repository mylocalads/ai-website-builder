faqs:
  - q: Can you just go over my old shingles?
    a: We do not, and we would push back on anyone offering to. Layering hides exactly what you are paying us to find — soft decking, rot at the chimney, split rafters. It also adds weight to a roof structure that was not designed for two roofs, and it shortens the life of the new shingles because they sit on an uneven surface and run hotter. Tear-off costs a little more on day one and is cheaper across the life of the roof.
  - q: What is the difference between three-tab and architectural shingles?
    a: Three-tab is a single flat layer with cut tabs — the cheapest option and the shortest-lived. Architectural shingles are laminated from two layers, so they are heavier, hold up better to wind, carry longer manufacturer warranties, and give a roof visible depth rather than a flat sheet. Most replacements we do now are architectural. The slate-look profile is an architectural shingle cut to imitate a slate course.
  - q: How long does it take?
    a: Most residential roofs in this area go on in a single day once the material is on site. Larger homes, steep pitches, and roofs that turn up widespread rotted decking can run into a second day. If we expect that, you hear it before we start rather than after.
  - q: How do I know if I need a new roof or just a repair?
    a: Granules washing into the gutters, shingles curling or going brittle, bare patches after a wind event, and leaks appearing in more than one plane are the signs a roof is at the end rather than in need of a patch. A single leak at a chimney or a vent boot usually is not. We get on the roof, photograph what we find, and tell you which one you have got.
