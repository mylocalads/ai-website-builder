long_description: Metal is the roof that makes the most sense in a snow climate and the one people ask about last. A properly installed metal roof sheds snow rather than holding it, which cuts the melt-and-refreeze cycle that drives ice dams at the eaves, and it will comfortably outlast two or three asphalt roofs put on in sequence. The installation is a different discipline from shingle work — panel layout, fastener pattern, thermal movement, and the flashing where panels meet a chimney or a wall all have to be planned before the first sheet goes up, because metal does not forgive being adjusted afterwards. We install it on houses, additions, porches and outbuildings, with the best materials we can specify for the job.

faqs:
  - q: Is metal worth the extra cost over asphalt?
    a: It depends on how long you plan to own the house. Metal costs more up front and lasts long enough to replace two or three asphalt roofs, so across decades it is generally the cheaper roof. Across five years it is not. We will give you both numbers and let you decide rather than steering you.
  - q: Does a metal roof help with ice dams?
    a: It helps. Snow slides off a metal panel rather than sitting on it and melting slowly, which reduces the melt-refreeze cycle that builds ice at the eaves. It is not a substitute for attic insulation and ventilation, and on a roof that already dams badly we would want to look at both.
  - q: Is it loud in the rain?
    a: Much less than people expect. A metal roof over solid decking and attic insulation is not meaningfully louder than shingle — the drumming people remember is from bare metal over open purlins on a barn.
